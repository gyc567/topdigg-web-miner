# Thinking Machines Lab Releases Inkling 2 Mini, an Open-Weight Model That Runs on a Laptop

> Today's main thread: models are splitting into tiers. On one side the models that actually run, 26.4B parameters, open weights, local on a laptop. On the other side the models that go big, 770B total parameters, 1M context, native multimodal MoE. The decision an operator should not make is which side to chase. The decision to make is figuring out which tier you are actually buying, because the accounting for local inference and the accounting for cloud inference are completely different problems.
> The second thread deserves even more attention: safety is becoming the product itself. The METR evaluation numbers, MiniMax's cryptanalysis breakthroughs, OpenHands letting an Agent auto-patch CVEs in its own images, all three say the same thing, that the boundary of capability is now something someone has to define and someone else has to absorb.

## [Model Releases and Updates]

### 1. Thinking Machines Lab Releases Inkling 2 Mini, an Open-Weight Model That Runs on a Laptop
Thinking Machines Lab released the open-weight model Inkling 2 Mini, using a recurrent Transformer architecture that runs the middle 12 layers three times per token, with 26.4B total parameters, 3.4B active, and roughly 20GB of memory usage, runnable locally on a Mac with 32GB of memory or more.

Source: [Thinking Machines Lab official blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)

The numbers that matter are 3.4B active and 20GB of memory, not 26.4B. Competition among open models has shifted from how many parameters a model has to whether it runs on your machine. If you are outfitting a team with local models, the purchasing question should change from how many billions to how much VRAM it eats, how many calls per day, and whether it needs to phone home. For anyone in the private deployment business, this is the most actionable item of the day.

### 2. Tencent Hunyuan Opens the Weights for Hy4
Tencent Hunyuan released the open weights for Hy4, positioned as a native multimodal flagship MoE model with 770B total parameters, 49B active, and support for 1M context. It shipped alongside a Hy4 System Card containing a safety evaluation report, with GitHub, Hugging Face weights, and GGUF download links, and the team will hold an AMA on r/LocalLLaMA this Friday from 8 to 10 AM PT.

Source: [X: Tencent Hunyuan (@TencentHunyuan)](https://x.com/TencentHunyuan/status/2123625549147070538)

1M context is the most concrete number of the day, and it beats most long-context marketing claims. Shipping the System Card alongside it also means open weights are starting to have a compliance template: prove safety first, then talk about downloads. For teams building model applications inside China, this one item is enough. The competition will still be fought among the domestic giants, and open weights are a means, not the destination.

### 3. Google Ships Gemini 4 Hydrogen, Now Live in the Gemini App and Search
Google announced that Gemini 4 Hydrogen is officially live, built around speed and everyday tasks, and now available in the Gemini app and in Search. The model is designed for a range of agentic workflows and emphasizes fast, efficient execution. Google says it follows instructions more tightly, calls tools more accurately, and produces reliable results with fewer back-and-forth exchanges.

Source: [X: Google (@Google)](https://x.com/Google/status/2123825847815561261)

Note the selling point about fewer back-and-forth exchanges. For anyone running a consumer product, translate it into one line: Google has admitted that conversation turns are themselves a cost, and is now compressing them. If your product only gets an answer after the user keeps pressing, that part of the experience will eventually be eaten by the platform default.

### 2. browser-use 1.0 Replaces 24 Browser Actions With a Single Python REPL Tool
Browser Use released browser-use 1.0, replacing the 24 browser actions of the 0.x line with a single persistent Python REPL plus native Chrome DevTools Protocol access.

Source: [Browser Use](https://browser-use.com/posts/browser-use-1-0)

Going from 24 actions to one tool interface is counterintuitive but correct. The more actions you expose, the more likely the model picks the wrong one. If your team is building an agent toolchain, this is a ready-made subtraction case. It is not about adding more APIs, it is about collapsing capability into one programmable loop.

Source: [Google Research official blog](https://research.google/blog/how-google-duplex-evolved-to-also-call-businesses-for-you-in-gemini/)

## [Research Papers]

### 1. Anthropic Mathematician Levent Alpöge Announces a Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis With Claude, Releases 17.37 Million Lines of Lean Code
On his personal website, Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge announced a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis made with Claude. There is no paper, only 14 archives totaling 175187 Lean files and 17.37 million lines of code.

Source: [WeChat account: Digital Life Kazik](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/8Zi3nfwddzFy6cZsK2_KxA)

No paper, just 17.37 million lines of Lean. The release format is itself the news: AI-assisted mathematical proof is replacing the readable paper with the executable proof. For teams that build code assets, the distribution format of verifiable artifacts is changing too.

### 2. METR Publishes the November 2026 Capability Measurement Update, AI R&D Capabilities Roughly Doubling Every 3.6 Months and Becoming Harder to Measure
METR published a capability evaluation update covering models up to Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, finding that model time horizons on TH1.1 and TH2 tasks roughly double every 3.6 months, while the doubling time for the verifiable subset is about 1.65 months.

Source: [METR official research blog](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/)

Doubling every 3.6 months while becoming harder to measure is the combination that matters. Capability is moving faster than evaluation can keep up. If you are setting expectations for your team based on public evaluations, know that these numbers have a shelf life of one or two quarters.

### 3. MiniMax Internal Model MIM Achieves Practical Attacks on Multiple Cryptanalysis Problems and Opens the Verification Traces
MiniMax's research team used its internal research model MIM to run automated cryptanalysis against modern cipher schemes, reporting several first practical breaks against NIST candidate algorithms, peer-reviewed schemes, and public challenge benchmarks where no practical attack had existed, and turning some theoretical attacks into executable ones.

Source: [MiniMax official blog](https://www.minimax.io/blog/practical-cryptanalysis-with-minimaxs-latest-internal-model)

The open verification traces are the most solid part of this story. Cryptanalysis is a field that had capability but no practical landing, and models have now pushed it past the practical threshold. Teams building security products should note that the speed gap between offense and defense is closing.

## [Tricks and Opinions]

### 1. Runway Explains How It Built an Agent-Driven Software Factory
Runway described its practice of progressively handing every stage of software delivery over to agents. In October agents drove 38% of merged pull requests, up from 0.1% in June. Median time to first approval fell from over two hours to about five minutes, and the rollback rate for PRs approved by AI alone is roughly one in a thousand, below the roughly one in two hundred for human-approved PRs.

Source: [Runway official blog](https://runwayml.com/news/engineering/building-a-software-factory)

Going from 0.1% to 38% in six months is a steeper slope than any AI coding tool advertisement. The rollback rate is the more telling number: PRs approved by AI alone roll back about one in a thousand, an order of magnitude better than human-approved work. The action is clear. Pick one low-risk production line, hand merge authority to the agent, and let data rather than belief set the rollout speed.

### 2. DeepSeek Harness Turns 100 Days Old, and the Author Shares 13 Practical Plugin Tricks
On the 100th day of the DeepSeek Harness release, Kazik shared 13 ways to use its plugins.

Source: [X: Kazik (@Khazix0918)](https://x.com/Khazix0918/status/2123627885730951241)

An open-source agent framework that can still produce a list of plugin tricks after 100 days shows the ecosystem moving toward tooling. The test for whether an agent platform is worth investing in is whether it created an environment other people want to write plugins for, which is more useful than a demo.

### 3. OpenHands Explains the Software Factory That Auto-Fixes CVEs in Its Own Images With an Agent
The OpenHands team detailed how it hands most CVE remediation work to an agent pipeline. The agent selects the fix, opens the pull request, merges low-risk items, and tracks them through to deployment, while a human reads one Slack message of at most eleven lines a day and a small number of pull requests.

Source: [OpenHands official blog](https://www.openhands.dev/blog/automating-cve-remediation-with-openhands)

A human reading one message of at most eleven lines a day is the most convincing description of AI deployment there is. It does not replace a role, it compresses the role into a single review action. For any process with clear acceptance criteria, such as operations, security, or support, this can be copied directly as an internal design.
