## Insights & Perspectives

### US DOJ Files Statement Supporting Fair Use for AI Training

The US Department of Justice filed a statement in the New York Times vs. OpenAI copyright case, arguing that training LLMs on copyrighted text generally constitutes fair use, warning that sweeping licensing requirements would weaken US AI competitiveness on national security grounds. Source: X (Rohan Paul)

### What is Harness Engineering? Google Demonstrates with ADK 2.0

Google's Shir Meir Lador introduces harness engineering—wrapping LLMs with deterministic components including orchestration layers, execution sandboxes, state persistence, and verification tools for safe autonomous code generation. Source: Google AI / DEV

### How to Write Reliable Rubrics for LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluations

Google AI team publishes a tutorial on writing reliable Boolean rubrics for LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations, noting that vague prompts lead to inconsistent evaluation and wasted tokens. Source: Google AI / DEV

### Anthropic Publishes E-Commerce Agent Architecture and Production Best Practices

Anthropic releases a guide for building e-commerce agents based on deployment experience with retail, travel, and telecom teams, centered on a single Claude in a standard Agent loop with skills and tools, and open-sources anthropics/commerce-agents. Source: Claude Blog

### How GitHub Copilot Reduces AI Coding Costs Without Sacrificing Quality

GitHub engineers share four cost-reduction changes: selective tool output compression, removing view tool line number prefixes, compressing task-tool prompts, and delivering results directly after background tasks complete. Source: GitHub Blog

### Google Summarizes 4 Engineering Patterns Behind Top AI Agents Challenge Submissions

Google reviews the AI Agents Challenge and extracts four engineering patterns from top submissions: bidirectional MCP, event-driven concurrency, standard fallback, and layered routing. Source: Google Developers Blog
