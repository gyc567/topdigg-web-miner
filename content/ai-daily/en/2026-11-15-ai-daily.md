# After Jensen Huang Left, the Open-Source Money Went First

> Today's thread: when one person leaves, what disappears is not authority but ecosystem funding. The 115,000-signature petition shows the old order is still clinging on, but the business owners have already started redoing the math.

## 【Industry News】

### 1. Musk and other executives sign petition calling on Nvidia to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO

The petition calling on Nvidia to restore its ousted CEO Jensen Huang has gathered more than 115,000 signatures as of Saturday afternoon, including 5,965 current employees, 1,108 former employees and 63,063 shareholders. Musk, Replit CEO Amjad Masad and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney have all signed.

Source: [TechCrunch：AI（RSS）](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/elon-musk-other-tech-execs-sign-petition-calling-on-nvidia-to-reinstate-jensen-huang/)

### 2. Cursor discloses security incident findings; malicious Claude Code extension affected up to 5,000 users

Cursor published the full investigation into its security incident: attackers exploited an exposed access key at Fireworks to read coding agent traces used for model training. API credentials for fewer than 0.01% of customers were exposed.

Source: [Cursor Blog](https://cursor.com/blog/security-incident-update)

### 3. How Asian governments responded after Jensen Huang left Nvidia

After Jensen Huang left Nvidia, governments across Asia responded in turn. South Korea's Ministry of Science said Nvidia reaffirmed it would supply 260,000 advanced GPUs to Korea and continue joint AI projects. Taiwan's Economics Minister Gong Ming-hsin said the move is unlikely to significantly change global AI industry growth, while Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry said it would closely watch Nvidia.

Source: [TechCrunch：AI（RSS）](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/asian-governments-react-to-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huangs-exit/)

### 4. Fed financial stability report: AI becomes Wall Street's top financial risk

The Federal Reserve published its financial stability report. A New York Fed survey found 87% of market participants now rank AI as the top financial risk, up from 50% in the spring. The report says market enthusiasm for superintelligence has inflated stock valuations; as of October, AI infrastructure debt exceeded $680 billion, about five times the full-year 2023 figure and roughly 31% of investment-grade corporate borrowing, while the largest banks' exposure to large tech, data center construction and financing vehicles approaches $1 trillion.

Source: [X：Rohan Paul (@rohanpaul_ai)](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2121391185499660332)
