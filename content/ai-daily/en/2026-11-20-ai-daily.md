# Anthropic Lists on Nasdaq, Opening at $111, 33% Above the IPO Price

> Today's main thread: the AI industry finally has a public market price. Anthropic opened at a $2 trillion valuation, and that is not a valuation story, it is an anchor. From now on every AI procurement, every compute contract, every SaaS price list has a public reference point. At the same time, the other side keeps cracking: Chinese tech firms are caught between halted chip shipments and closed overseas cloud routes. The lesson for operators: the model layer now has a valuation anchor, while the infrastructure layer is still getting more expensive.

## [Product Releases and Updates]

### 1. Gemini App for Mac Adds Computer Use, Can Execute Cross-App Tasks on Your Behalf

Google Labs VP Josh Woodward announced that the Gemini app for Mac has added computer use, letting it execute tasks across folders, apps and websites on the user's behalf, while screen and cursor control remain with the user. The official demo shows Gemini clicking, scrolling and typing in background windows, turning a sketch into a rigged 3D model in Blender.

Source: [X: Josh Woodward (VP, Google Labs)](https://x.com/joshwoodward/status/2123534862569324703)

"Control remains with the user" is the three words that matter here. Every desktop agent is fighting over the same question: how much authority does the user actually surrender. Gemini's answer is to keep final ownership of screen and cursor while the AI works in background windows. Worth copying into your own systems: give the agent freedom to act, but keep the last click human.

### 2. Google Launches Tool Retrieval Preview in the Gemini API

Google introduced Tool Retrieval in preview in the Interactions API of the Gemini API, letting agents load tool parameter schemas on demand instead of sending the full tool library with every request, reducing input tokens and preserving selection accuracy as tool counts grow.

Source: [Google Blog: AI (RSS)](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-api-tool-retrieval/)

More tools, more mistakes: that is the shared pain of every agent system. Google's fix is to change tool definitions from "ship the whole library every time" to "retrieve on demand", which is really just saving context cost. If your team is building an agent toolchain, this one is worth borrowing directly.

## [Industry News]

### 1. Anthropic Lists on Nasdaq, Opening at $111, 33% Above the IPO Price

Anthropic completed its IPO on Nasdaq, trading at $111.50 within minutes of the open, 33% above the $83 offering price, which itself came in above the company's earlier $70 to $78 range. The offering raised more than $81 billion, and combined with concurrent private financing, cash reserves are expected to reach $220 billion. At the opening price the market cap exceeds $2 trillion, and CEO Dario Amodei used his opening remarks to reaffirm the company's commitment to safety.

Source: [TechCrunch: AI (RSS)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-hits-nasdaq-exchanges-at-110-27-above-ipo-price/)

### 2. Nadella Explains Buyouts for 60,000 Employees: Becoming an AI-First Organization, Not Layoffs

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella explained at the DealBook Summit that offering buyouts to 60,000 employees in October was about giving people the choice during the transition rather than replacing them with AI. The goal is for everyone to work with AI and shift toward managing AI agents.

Source: [TechCrunch: AI (RSS)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-explains-why-he-asked-60k-employees-to-quit/)

This runs directly against the "AI replaces jobs" narrative and deserves a separate look. Nadella's framing is not about cutting people, but about letting each of them decide whether to stay, with the rest shifting to managing AI agents. Translated into organizational language: the future has one layer that does the work and one layer that manages AI doing the work, and demand grows for the second. If your headcount plan still assumes the old structure, next year's org chart will be wrong.

### 3. Chip Halts and Closed Overseas Cloud Routes Hit Chinese Tech Firms Twice Over

After the US Commerce Department tightened AI chip export controls and shut off third-country cloud compute channels, several large Asian AI data center deals began to collapse. Moonshot AI canceled a $150 million contract with an Indonesian cloud provider for an Nvidia Blackwell Ultra B300 cluster to train Kimi K4, having prepaid roughly 50% of the value versus the industry standard of 15%. TSMC and Samsung have suspended delivery of some processors to Chinese customers pending licenses; ByteDance and Baidu Kunlun are affected, and the removal of exemptions for non-AI chips also reaches smartphone processors from Xiaomi, Oppo and vivo.

Source: [The Decoder: AI News (RSS)](https://the-decoder.com/chinese-tech-firms-face-a-double-hit-as-chip-shipments-stop-and-overseas-cloud-access-disappear/)

This is the hardest-hitting item today for anyone building in China. Three layers of damage: chips you cannot get, overseas cloud you cannot rent, and contracts that get canceled on a whim. Moonshot prepaid 50% and still got canceled, which shows these deals carry no default protection at all. Swapping one chip vendor for another does not solve it, because the restriction targets categories, not companies. If you run AI infrastructure, the next six months of renegotiations are about payment guarantees and delivery alternatives, not price.

### 4. Nvidia Stock Review: Strong Earnings, Yet Down Nearly 40% From October Highs

The Verge traces Nvidia's recent stock moves: third-quarter revenue doubled year over year to $115.8 billion, data center grew 110%, and the Vera Rubin platform contributed nearly $20 billion in revenue. But amid a broad chip selloff, the departure of former CEO Jensen Huang and company governance investigations, the stock traded near $150, nearly 40% below its October 6 record high, closing Thursday at $145.98, a 52-week low.

Source: [The Verge: AI (RSS)](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1038063/nvidia-stock-market-price-earnings)

Revenue doubled and the stock halved. Both are true, which means the market is no longer paying a growth premium, it is pricing governance. The people buying Nvidia hardware and the people renting Nvidia compute are in different positions now: the latter are barely affected, while the former carry the valuation drawdown of the entire AI capex narrative.

## [Techniques and Opinions]

### 1. OpenAI Explains How the Codex-Powered Performance Factory Keeps ChatGPT Fast

OpenAI detailed its Performance Factory system, which uses Codex agents in the cloud to automatically diagnose and fix ChatGPT performance regressions. Its Regression Workflow tracked more than 1,700 investigations and pinpointed over 600 root causes over three months, roughly 17 automatic investigations per day.

Source: [OpenAI: Official Updates (RSS, excluding enterprise and customer cases)](https://openai.com/index/how-openai-performance-factory-keeps-chatgpt-fast)

Seventeen automatic performance investigations a day is something most teams could actually build. If you have monitoring alerts, a ticketing system and a code repository, then "let an agent investigate the alert, localize the cause and open a fix PR" is a ready-made pipeline. SaaS operators can plan around this directly: pick the single most painful production metric, hand the debugging to the agent, and keep humans on review.
