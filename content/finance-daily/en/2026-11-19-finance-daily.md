The most striking thing on the screen today was not how far indices fell, but that **precious metals simply collapsed in the face of rate hike expectations**: spot gold fell back to $3,743.88 (-2.73%) overnight, and spot silver dropped to $45.61 (-3.94%). Meanwhile NVDA broke below $151 (-2.90%) with an RSI(14) of only 20.8, close to textbook oversold.

All three point to the same cause: **the October Fed minutes published on November 18 said that "almost all participants" judged a further increase in the target range "likely appropriate", and CME FedWatch now puts the probability of a 25 basis point hike on December 9 at roughly 95%, with a 50 basis point move at about 5%.**

One sentence conclusion: **a 25 basis point hike on December 9 is close to settled, so that is no longer a suspense item. The real suspense is whether market pricing has already run further ahead of the Fed than the Fed itself will.**

## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

**🦅 The Fed: the hike is effectively settled, the dot plot is the suspense**
On October 28 the FOMC held the federal funds target range at 3.75-4.00 percent by a vote of 10 to 2, the first pause after the 25 basis point hike on September 16, with Cook and Hammack voting to add another 25 basis point. Chair Kevin Warsh has now been in the seat for several months. **The October minutes published on November 18 state explicitly that "almost all participants" judged a further increase "likely appropriate", with a couple noting that if incoming inflation data proves unfavourable, a move larger than 25 basis points could be worth considering.** CME FedWatch puts the probability of a 25 basis point hike on December 9 at about 95% and 50 basis points at about 5%, and just a week earlier on November 6 the 25 basis point probability was only 76.8%. **A jump of nearly 20 percentage points inside one week is the source of every wobble in this week's market.** The next decisive data points are the October PCE on November 25 and the November payrolls report on December 4.

**Read:** **Whether the Fed hikes on December 9 has no trading value left. What does have value is the new dot plot released alongside it (end-2027, end-2028 and end-2029 terminal rates).** If the dot plot pushes the 2027 median higher, the valuation anchor for the entire market gets hoisted again and growth stocks take another hit. If the dot plot shows the end of the hiking cycle approaching, you get a relief rally instead. **So what owners should be doing now is not judging whether a hike happens, but judging whether their industry still holds pricing power in a world where rates step up again.** If your customers settle on your quoted pricing cycle, **get a repricing window written into the contract from now through the first quarter of next year, instead of waiting for rates to peak before you negotiate.**

**🏰 The European Central Bank: steady rates, watching, until December 17**
The deposit facility rate stands at a latest reading of 2.50 percent, unchanged at the October 29 meeting. **This year the ECB has moved out of its cutting cycle into a new phase of "steady observation, data-driven" policy**, with the core aim of balancing rate stability, inflation control and growth support. The next rate decision is December 17, at 2:15pm Frankfurt time, which is 9:15pm Beijing time that day.

**Read:** The ECB holding while the Fed hikes is the single biggest source of pressure on the euro, and it is also why the euro only managed a soft 0.19 percent decline today rather than a break. **There is simply no money left in the market that wants a stronger euro.** For owners, the ECB's wait-and-see stance is actually good news: **energy prices and funding costs stay in a manageable range and European demand has not broken.** If you run European channels or expansion projects, this level of the exchange rate works in your favour. **Watch December 17: if the ECB turns hawkish at that meeting, the euro has one more leg down.**

**🇨🇳 The People's Bank of China: LPR on hold for a long stretch, with rate and RRR cuts as targeted drip-feeding**
The one-year LPR sits at 3.0 percent and the five-year-plus LPR at 3.5 percent, unchanged for consecutive months since the 10 basis point cut on May 20, 2025. **The policy stance remains "a moderately accommodative monetary policy", but the language stresses "flexible and efficient use of tools such as RRR cuts and rate cuts to keep liquidity ample", with the emphasis on precise drip-feeding rather than flooding the market.** The constraint is equally clear: the net interest margin of commercial banks has been compressed to around 1.42 percent, and capital profit ratios have fallen from 9.57 percent in the first quarter of 2024 to 8.18 percent, so banks themselves have little incentive to push LPR quotes lower.

**Read:** **The question facing the People's Bank of China was never whether it wants to loosen, but whether the banks can still afford to.** With margins squeezed to 1.42 percent, transmission of a rate cut is increasingly hard for banks to carry out on their own. The direct implication for owners: **do not expect a surprise on your borrowing costs, and do not build expansion plans around waiting for a cut.** The only approach that does not depend on policy favours is to **structure your capital as long-dated locked-in cheap debt plus a thicker operating cash buffer.** **And with the yuan steady near 6.67, businesses earning dollars while paying in renminbi are sitting on a real foreign exchange gain. Do not waste it.**

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Big Events

**China-US leaders and diplomacy:**
On November 11 Wang Yi held a phone call with US Secretary of State Rubio, exchanging views on China-US relations and interaction between the two countries' leaders, with the Chinese side proposing stronger communication, proper handling of differences and sensitive issues, and mutual support for the 2026 APEC leaders' meeting and the G20 summit. **On November 13 the China-US trade council under the economic and trade consultation mechanism held its first meeting by video conference**, covering tariffs, the reciprocal tariff reduction framework of 30 billion against 30 billion, agricultural trade and other topics. The same day Wang Yi met in Beijing with the US delegation attending the 2026 China-US Party Dialogue. **On November 10 the USTR announced in the Federal Register that it will continue extending the 178 existing Section 301 tariff exclusions on Chinese goods until January 9, 2027.** The same day the US Defense Department announced the removal of WuXi AppTec from the Chinese military companies list, and on November 9 the BIS extended the suspension of the "Affiliated Entities Rule" expanding end-user controls to January 10, 2027.

**Read:** **Taken together, this cluster of actions points to buying time, not preparing a showdown.** The 178 exclusions extended to January next year, the export control expansion suspended to January, WuXi AppTec delisted, these are all signals of deliberately loosening the pressure on commercial entities during a negotiation window. **For friends running cross-border supply chains, the next two months are the most certain phase of the exclusion window, and it is worth recalculating tariff costs on your key HS codes. Get the orders that can move early out of the door rather than holding them until year end.** Just do not read "frozen" as "cancelled". The assessment from Fudan University's development institute is accurate: an extension is both a buffer and a preparation period, and there is still a long road to substantive resolution.

**China-Europe trade:**
Following the Stockholm talks between China and the US, both sides continue to push for the scheduled extension of the suspended 24 percent reciprocal tariff and China's countermeasures, with tariffs entering a phased "freeze". **The current American strategy is issue stacking plus deliberate policy ambiguity**: running tariffs, capacity, energy and rare earth export issues in parallel while keeping the final outcome uncertain, turning tariff extensions, energy exemptions and technology export arrangements into adjustable bargaining chips, **and concentrating effort first on pushing through tariff and market arrangements with the EU, Japan, Vietnam and the Philippines, fixing rules on the periphery before importing the result into talks with China.**

**Read:** **This is the part that owners actually need to track.** The EU now faces a choice between accepting the rules the US has set and being taxed separately, and **once the EU arrangement lands, the regulatory environment for Chinese exports into Europe gets rewritten, rather than waiting for China to join the talks.** Here is one concrete instruction for teams running European business: **go ask your customers right now what the cost curve of your European competitors looks like under the new US and EU rules.** That one piece of information determines whether you quote next, and at what price.

**Geopolitics:**
The Strait of Hormuz previously carried roughly one fifth of global energy transport and is currently subject to partial Iranian blockade. **Officials from Qatar and Iran held talks on the 17th, and Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araghchi visited Oman to discuss guaranteeing shipping passage through the Strait of Hormuz.** Reporting in the WSJ notes that, stimulated by the conflict in Iran, Gulf producers are racing to plan new routes bypassing the strait, and that the Hormuz crisis is permanently reshaping the oil map.

**Read:** **Notice the contradictory combination: geopolitics this tight, and gold still falling.** That confirms rates rather than hedging is the dominant variable for this round of precious metal pricing, as explained earlier. But the reverse also holds: **if the strait talks genuinely break down and a real blockade emerges, energy prices become the last straw on inflation and push the Fed even more hawkish, and only then does the hedging attribute of precious metals switch back on.** For owners, the practical advice is this: **if you operate in an energy-cost-sensitive industry (chemicals, logistics, manufacturing, cross-border e-commerce first-leg freight), run a stress test right now with oil up 25 percent and freight up 15 percent, rather than doing the arithmetic after the fact.** That exercise costs an hour and could be worth millions.
