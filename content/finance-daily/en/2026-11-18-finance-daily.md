## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

**🦅 The Federal Reserve**
The federal funds benchmark stays near 3.75 percent. The 2026 FOMC calendar has reached its October hold, and the next meeting is December 8 to 9, the final one of the year, with the rate decision and the latest economic projections released at 2 pm Eastern on December 9 and the chair's press conference at 2:30 pm. The Fed hiked 25 basis points in September and held in October.
**Read:** This is the date that matters most right now. The market has been stuck at 3.75 percent for a full year, and the December meeting either delivers a clear path down or writes "higher for longer" into the projections. **For any company with dollar borrowing, cross-border settlement or dollar assets, December 9 is a date you must keep a decision window open for.** Do not stack important contracts and funding arrangements on that week.

**🏰 The European Central Bank**
The ECB's core mandate is price stability in the euro area and preserving the purchasing power of the single currency. Its most recent policy meeting left the rate decision unchanged, with the focus on rebalancing the inflation outlook against economic growth.
**Read:** The ECB is in a harder spot than the Fed. Energy prices are hostage to geopolitics, inflation keeps recurring and growth is weak, so policy room is squeezed from both sides. For operators with meaningful exposure to Europe, **the question is not whether the ECB hikes but whether the euro breaks 1.10 against the dollar.** If it does, start converting part of your European revenue into dollars, because every step down in the euro is a real cost to export orders.

**🇨🇳 The People's Bank of China**
The Loan Prime Rate has been unchanged for nine consecutive months, with the market generally expecting another 5 to 10 basis points of cuts within the year. Monetary policy keeps its "stability first" tone.
**Read:** A flat LPR does not mean money is not loosening. What is actually doing the work is structural tools and money market prices, not the headline number. **The more useful angle for an operator is whether your own bank's corporate lending rate has actually come down and whether payment terms have stretched.** After nine flat months the next adjustment is more likely timed to fiscal and property policy than to broad easing, so do not wait for a flood.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Global Events

**China and the United States**
From September 23 to 25, 2026, President Xi Jinping paid a state visit to the United States, where the two heads of state exchanged views on a constructive strategic relationship and major international and regional issues and reached an eight-point consensus, including the agreement to build a constructive strategic relationship between China and the United States based on respect, fairness and parity. On November 13, the two sides held the first meeting of the trade council under the China-US economic and trade consultation mechanism by video conference, guided by the important consensus of the leaders' meeting, and exchanged candid, in-depth and constructive views on tariffs, the "30 billion for 30 billion" reciprocal tariff reduction arrangement, agricultural trade and other topics of mutual concern.
**Read:** An eight-point consensus plus a working trade council means the relationship has entered a phase of framework and rhythm rather than living on the edge of collapse. **But be clear-eyed: a mechanism is not an outcome, and the execution details in the rounds ahead are the real commercial variable.** Putting the "30 billion for 30 billion" figure on the table on November 13 is about as concrete as this process has been in a year, and it is worth tracking item by item.

**China and Europe**
The EU's final anti-dumping determination on Chinese tyres has landed, imposing duties of 4.3 to 45.3 percent. Europe is the largest export market for Chinese passenger car tyres, accounting for more than 40 percent of total exports, with more than 130 million units worth about 3.5 billion dollars shipped to Europe in 2025, and the sector has fragmented sharply after the ruling. At the same time the EU cancelled 84.2 percent of tariffs on certain Chinese goods, meaning Chinese solar glass within the new rules no longer carries those two trade remedy duties, and EU customs will refund the anti-dumping and countervailing duties collected during the review period, without interest.

**Read:** One up and one down is the EU's precision playbook. **It protects the high-tech capacity it lacks and strikes the sectors where it has excess.** Anyone selling into Europe should stop talking about "EU tariffs" in the abstract and check line by line against HS codes, because the same product category can simultaneously carry a new duty and a cancelled one. **And if your goods were ever subject to provisional double duties, the refund process can start now. Do not let it expire.**

**Geopolitics**
On November 15, Iran's Tasnim news agency reported that Kashkav, spokesperson for the national security and foreign policy committee of Iran's parliament, said the committee had completed its professional review of the plan relating to the Strait of Hormuz and had reached agreement with two littoral states on the relevant shipping channel. At the same time Iran keeps its self-description of the Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman as being in a state of conflict, preserving its self-defence and control levers, producing a dual narrative of temporary easing for shipping alongside a conflict framing.
**Read:** This is the most important undercurrent in the energy market this year. A temporary passage arrangement for shipping is a valve on freight costs that can be tightened at any moment. **If you ship out of the Middle East, or if freight is a meaningful share of your logistics cost, lock in some of the freight and transit time now** rather than waiting for the rules to change again.
