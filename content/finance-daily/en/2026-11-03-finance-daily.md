## 🏛 Central Banks & Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve**: The October 28 FOMC meeting held the federal funds target range at 3.75%-4.00%, the second consecutive hold after the 25bp hike in September. Minutes show policymakers raised their median projections for US GDP growth and core PCE inflation for 2026, 2027 and 2028, while noting that "the implications of developments in the Middle East remain unclear." Chair Warsh said the current policy stance is appropriate and that he has no intention of leaving the Fed before the Department of Justice investigation concludes.
> Commentary: A calm outcome with a tense interior. Raising growth and inflation projections together says the economy is fine but prices refuse to come down, which makes December's decision entirely dependent on jobs data. Warsh's "no intention of leaving" reassures the market, but it also means nobody is going to change policy stance in the near term. Anyone betting on cuts can take a break.

🏰 **European Central Bank**: The October 29 meeting held rates steady, the first pause after back-to-back hikes in June and September. The ECB's latest projections keep 2026 euro area inflation at 3%, raise 2027 to 2.5%, and nudge 2026 and 2027 growth to 0.9% and 1.4% respectively.
> Commentary: Slower hiking is a mid-cycle break, not a pivot. Raising growth and inflation projections together says the ECB is confident in the recovery, but the recovery itself is pushing prices up. "Recovering while worrying about inflation" is the classic recipe for a range-bound euro, with buyers and sellers fighting above 1.12.

🇨🇳 **People's Bank of China**: LPR stayed flat again at 3.0% for one year and 3.5% for five years and above, both at historic lows. The main thrust is reforming the pricing benchmark, with market attention shifting from the LPR numbers themselves toward a potential transfer of the key policy rate to the 7-day reverse repo rate. The swap facility introduced earlier continues to provide emergency liquidity to non-bank institutions facing large redemptions.
> Commentary: No rate cut does not mean no easing. The toolkit is moving from "cut rates" toward "reshape the framework plus top up liquidity," which is a more refined approach. On a day the STAR 50 fell over 4%, the existence of such PBoC tools is itself a cushion.

## 🌐 Diplomacy & Major Events

**US-China Leaders' Diplomacy**: The US-China trade framework continues along the "truce maintained + institutional implementation" track. The results of the ninth round of talks were endorsed by both countries' leaders, who agreed to establish a China-US Trade Council under the existing economic and trade consultation mechanism to optimize bilateral trade, and reached a "$30 billion" reciprocal tariff reduction arrangement, while the Kuala Lumpur consultation outcomes were extended. Implementation instructions have been handed to the relevant departments.
> Commentary: Making the tariff cut "reciprocal" rather than unilateral is the core design language of this deal. It gives both sides a story their domestic politics can live with, and it means any future escalation looks like tearing up a mutual commitment. The economic line won't break near term, but a truce is never a reconciliation, and border friction becomes normal.

**China-EU Trade**: China-EU trade defense instruments remain in active use, with Europe's countervailing duties on Chinese electric vehicles and photovoltaic products still in force, and the overcapacity dispute handled through dialogue under the framework of "consensus plus restrictions." The EU's latest round of China trade policy review and countermeasures is being mulled.
> Commentary: The China-EU friction has long since shifted from an all-out tariff war to precise, targeted defense. Less damage, but stickier. Europe buys industrial breathing room with quotas and minimum-price mechanisms; China buys market access with price and overseas capacity. This isn't one side winning, it's pushing the problem forward. Forward to whom? To the next industry cycle.

**Geopolitics**: Signs of Middle East de-escalation pushed crude oil down more than 5% in a single session, visibly unwinding the risk premium. The corridor that allowed Gulf states to export roughly 1.5 billion barrels of crude with more than 3,000 commercial vessels safely transiting the Strait of Hormuz under U.S. support remains open. Crude's slide from $109 to $96 is a textbook repricing of geopolitical premium. The Russia-Ukraine conflict continues, and calls for direct talks have yet to yield substantive progress.
> Commentary: A 5% one-day drop in oil is the market repricing geopolitical premium it had previously priced in. Don't cheer too early, though: $96 isn't cheap. The thing to watch is that if Hormuz transit volumes drop again, that 5% can be given back in two days. Risk premium has one defining trait: it holds a grudge.
