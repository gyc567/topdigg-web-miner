The thing worth watching today is not how far indices fell, but that **three things happened at once**: precious metals broke down harder, an AI leader posted record results and still fell, and the major central banks of the world collectively turned toward rate hikes.

Spot gold broke below 3,610 dollars (-3.48 percent) and silver slid to 42.76 dollars (-5.98 percent), putting it at the **second consecutive session of a double digit scale decline**. NVDA closed at 145.98 dollars (-3.30 percent) with an RSI(14) of only 19.64, which is **textbook oversold** on a technical reading. It had just reported revenue up 93 percent and net profit up 128 percent. Anthropic listed on the market with a record debut at a 2 trillion dollar valuation, yet US equities fell across the board, with the Dow down 560 points and the Nasdaq down 551 points.

**The conclusion first:** this is not an AI bubble bursting. It is **the anchor of global pricing switching from a rate cut trade to a rate hike trade**. Gold fell because real rates are rising, NVDA fell because the long duration asset it represents is the most fragile under a rising discount rate, and the fact that the broader market fell on the Anthropic debut says the headwind no longer sits at the single stock level but at the liquidity level.

It is worth hedging next year's order exposure in tranches rather than betting on a one way renminbi move.**

## 📈 US Equities Focus

🍎 **Apple (AAPL)**: closed at **$349.21** (-0.92%), market cap **$5.07 trillion**, trailing price earnings **38.85**, analyst target price **$345.63**, RSI(14) **52.96**.

**Read:** The target price now sits **below the current price** (-0.94 percent), which means the sell side has started to argue Apple is overvalued. But note what that number is not saying: revenue of 478.8 billion (+15.1 percent), net profit of 132.2 billion (+18.0 percent), EPS of 8.98 (+20.4 percent). **The fundamentals have not broken, the valuation has simply run out of room.** Paying 38.85 times earnings for a business growing at 20 percent has to be compressed when the discount rate is rising. **For holders the implication is direct: Apple is now a defensive position rather than an offensive one, and its value sits in cash flow and dividend rather than in imagination.**

🚗 **Tesla (TSLA)**: closed at **$374.41** (-2.43%), market cap **$1.48 trillion**, trailing price earnings **411.36**, forward price earnings 175.23, analyst target price **$412.48**, RSI(14) **47.23**, beta **1.74**.

The target of 412.48 is 10 percent above the current price, **but note that this "10 percent premium" is being handed out at 411 times earnings, meaning the market believes it can become another 10 percent more expensive.** **For owners the read is simple: in this rate environment Tesla is a high volatility position, not an allocation.**

🔥 **NVIDIA (NVDA)**: closed at **$145.98** (-3.30%), market cap **$3.56 trillion** (down 21.6 percent over the year), trailing price earnings **15.82**, forward price earnings 10.15, analyst target price **$246.67**, RSI(14) **19.64**, beta **2.25**.

**Read:** **This is the most counterintuitive line in the whole dataset: revenue of 361.8 billion (+93.3 percent), net profit of 226.3 billion (+128.1 percent), EPS of 9.31 (+130.4 percent), and the stock is falling at 15.82 times earnings.** The reason is not in the earnings report, it is in two other places. First, **the news that Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive took apart the premium attached to "Nvidia equals Huang"**, and the market now worries about execution in the post Huang era. Second, **a rising discount rate systematically compresses long duration assets**, and a beta of 2.25 means it falls 2.25 percent for every 1 percent the market falls.

The analyst target of 246.67 implies **there is still 67 percent of room**. An RSI of 19.64 implies **there is strong technical rebound momentum**. Both numbers existing at once is the most honest description of the current trading structure: **it is a combination of oversold conditions, a cheap valuation and a damaged narrative.** That makes it a short term rebound candidate, not a signal that the bottom is in. **If your portfolio is overweight AI, the action is not to add. It is to convert the position from conviction into discipline.**

## 🥇 🥈 Commodities

**Gold:** spot **$3,609.92/oz** (-3.48 percent), with an intraday high of 3,626.74 and low of 3,600.79. Price sits near a 14 month low, **down roughly 15 percent over 12 weeks**.

**Silver:** spot **$42.76/oz** (-5.98 percent), with an intraday low of 42.46.

**Read:** **Gold and silver falling this hard while geopolitics stay tense and the Strait of Hormuz remains partially blocked is itself the most important signal of the day.** For the past half year, geopolitical fear was the pillar holding up gold. That pillar has now broken, which tells you **the dominant pricing variable has switched to real rates rather than safe haven demand.** The logic is straightforward: a 25 basis point hike raises the opportunity cost of holding a zero yield asset, and every non income producing asset gets drained at the same time.

**Silver falling 5.98 percent against gold at 3.48 percent deserves more attention**, because silver is half an industrial metal and is carrying manufacturing demand expectations on top of that. **For owners the practical advice is specific: if you operate in an energy or raw material cost sensitive industry (chemicals, logistics, manufacturing, cross border e-commerce first leg freight), run an oil up 25 percent and freight up 15 percent stress test right now.** The exercise costs an hour and getting it wrong can cost millions.

**The second thing worth doing:** if you have been meaning to allocate to precious metals, **the window for building in tranches is right here, not "after another leg down"**. The reason is simple. The slope of the hikes is settled (a December hike is very likely), but **after** the hikes land, the pressure of real rates on gold is released once rather than every day. **Waiting for a complete washout at a level where 90 percent of the bad news is already priced rarely works.**

## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **The Fed:** the October FOMC minutes published on November 18 showed that **almost all participants judged a further increase in the target range likely appropriate**, and the minutes explicitly noted that **officials saw risks tied to AI**, with a larger increase still on the table. On November 18, Waller said publicly that the central bank may need to **shock people with a 50 basis point hike**, and insisted on a large move in December. **JPMorgan said on November 19 that the Fed will hike at the next three meetings as inflation persists.** CME FedWatch puts the probability of a 25 basis point hike on December 9 at roughly 95 percent, and a 50 basis point move at about 5 percent.

**Read:** **Going from a rate cut trade to three consecutive hikes is a complete narrative reversal, and the market did it in a matter of days.** Waller's "shock people" line is the key signal, because **it means the Fed has moved from passively reacting to inflation toward actively demonstrating resolve.** The slope of tightening will be steeper than the past two years. For owners the implication is concrete: **your dollar funding costs, the valuation of your dollar assets and global risk appetite for the next twelve months all have to be recalculated on this new anchor.** The floor on every asset price over the coming year is decided at the December 9 meeting.

🏰 **The European Central Bank:** Reuters reported exclusively that **the ECB will discuss raising reserve requirements at next week's meeting**, with ING describing the move as bringing funding pressure closer. At the same time **the euro fell to a 19 month low against the dollar on November 19 while euro area inflation reached 4 percent**.

**Read:** **Inflation at 4 percent paired with a currency at a 19 month low, and the central bank is still discussing tightening, tells you the ECB's inflation pressure comes mainly from the supply and energy side, and it has chosen to respond with the one lever it still has.** For anyone selling into Europe: **European asset valuations (property, cyclical stocks, European banks) need to come down, the marginal cost of European demand is rising, and the window for pricing is narrowing.** More practically: if your European customers are still squeezing your price, **now is a point where you can push back with some firmness**, because their position is tighter than yours.

🇨🇳 **The PBOC:** the 1 year LPR held at 3.0 percent and the 5 year and above LPR held at 3.5 percent, unchanged for months since the May 2025 cut. **But the real action sat on the foreign exchange side: on November 3 the PBOC announced an increase in the foreign exchange reserve requirement, the first in five years, in order to slow a too rapid renminbi appreciation**, a move confirmed by Nikkei Asia on November 15.

**Read:** **The PBOC line is clear: moderately loose monetary policy and a renminbi that does not appreciate too fast, both at the same time.** Translated into practice for anyone doing cross border business: **there is a ceiling on renminbi appreciation, so do not sign long term dollar priced contracts betting on one way appreciation, and do not panic convert either. Locking part of next year's exposure around 6.68 is the steady option.** Separately, **with the Fed set to hike three times in a row, domestic rates are unlikely to move.** Bank net interest margins have already been squeezed to around 1.42 percent, so **your funding costs will not surprise you soon. Do not build an expansion plan on waiting for a cut.** What to do instead is structure funds as long dated locked cheap debt plus a fat operating cash buffer.

## 🌍 Diplomacy and Major Events

**US China leader diplomacy:**
President Trump arrived in Beijing on November 18 for trade talks and a leader level meeting. **The first meeting of the trade council under the US China economic and trade consultation mechanism was held on November 13**, covering tariffs, reciprocal tariff reduction arrangements and agricultural trade. However, reporting on November 20 shows **questions remain over agricultural purchase commitments in the US China talks.** Reuters reported on November 17 that **Chinese officials said chip equipment export restrictions threaten the EU China trade thaw**.

**Read:** **The rhythm is talk first, sign later, with deliberate blanks left in, not a single package landing at once.** Agricultural purchases matter because they are the **easiest to quantify, the easiest to deliver and the most visible to voters.** Beijing explicitly tying chip equipment restrictions to threatening the trade thaw means **technology controls remain a card in Chinese hands rather than a concession made.** **One honest word for anyone in exports: whatever tariff reductions actually land on paper will most likely be concentrated in consumer goods and agriculture. Do not budget premium equipment and chip related lines at zero duty before the first half of 2027.**

**EU China trade:** the EU moved repeatedly in the middle of November. **On November 16 the EU imposed anti dumping duties on certain cold rolled flat steel products, on November 17 the Commission announced additional tariffs on some steel products and reinforced its trade toolbox, and on November 19 the EU imposed a 16 percent anti dumping duty on Vietnamese steel**, while **EU China trade negotiations continued between Brussels and Beijing**.

**Read:** **The EU's moves point to a clear strategy: no soybeans, no aircraft, steel and anti dumping first.** The reason is not hard to see. **While the Fed pushes global funding costs higher, the EU needs to protect its own manufacturing base**, and that is what every major economy will be doing in the second half of 2026. **Vietnam has already been named, and that is a direct warning to every "shift capacity to Southeast Asia" story.** For anyone operating across borders, **if part of your cost advantage comes from the "relocate to Vietnam or Southeast Asia" narrative, you need to redo that arithmetic, because EU anti dumping duties are turning this route into a precisely targetable one.** Concretely: check the current EU duty rate on your HS code, and check whether your Vietnam or Southeast Asian suppliers are also on the list.

**Geopolitics:** **on November 19, Trump, Xi and Putin held a 90 minute trilateral meeting in Beijing and issued a joint declaration on peace, but the declaration did not mention the war in Ukraine or Iran.** Trump said on November 18 that reaching an agreement limiting nuclear weapons would be hard, with Russia showing limited interest on nuclear issues. **IAEA Director General Grossi said on November 18 that the planned US Saudi nuclear arrangement on inspections was very satisfactory.**

**Read:** **The value of that declaration lies not in its content but in the fact that the three met in frame at all.** Leaving Ukraine and Iran out means **those three fronts were deliberately left blank, held back as future bargaining chips rather than already settled.** And the very phrase "joint declaration on peace" tells you **the three sides had not even agreed on how to describe the competition.**

**The most practical point for owners: the nuclear risk is unresolved, which means the energy risk premium is still sitting in oil rather than in gold.** It is notable that gold collapsed at the same time, because **the market has temporarily chosen to believe tightening overwhelms safe haven demand. But if the Strait of Hormuz talks genuinely break down and a real blockade emerges, oil becomes the last straw on inflation and pushes the Fed even further hawkish, and only then does the hedging attribute of precious metals switch back on.** **That is why holding "oil up" alongside "gold down" is internally consistent, but holding "gold up" and "growth stocks up" at the same time is not.**
