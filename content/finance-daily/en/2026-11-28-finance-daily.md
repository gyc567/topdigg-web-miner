- **USD/JPY**: 159.580 (+0.18%), intraday range 159.14-159.594
- **GBP/USD**: 1.3099 (-0.06%)
- **USD/CNH (offshore)**: around 6.66, onshore yuan at 6.6541

The dollar is pressing toward 159.6 against the yen, one step from 160. The euro has dropped below the 1.0950 handle. With all three major currencies soft and the dollar only slightly firmer, the market is waiting for a clear direction rather than actively betting on one.

**Takeaway**: What matters is not the exchange rate itself but the Dec 8-9 Fed decision and the next round of China-US trade talks in Paris. If you collect dollar receivables or buy overseas, directional hedging is not worth the cost right now. Line up the timing of your dollar receipts and payments instead of making a decision on the day of the meeting.

🇨🇳 **Chinese assets (Nov 27 close)**

- **Shanghai Composite**: 3606.49 (+3.73%), turnover around 930 billion yuan
- **Shenzhen Component**: 10814.02 (+7.24%), turnover around 1.07 trillion yuan
- **ChiNext Index**: 2443.96 (+11.61%)
- **STAR 50**: 1346.25 (+15.07%), the third largest single-day gain on record

More than 5,200 stocks rose and over 200 hit the daily limit, led by domestic AI compute and semiconductors.

**Takeaway**: This was not an ordinary rebound, it was a repricing driven by a policy signal. The State Council executive meeting used the term "national team" for the first time in its official readout, calling for stepping up market stabilisation efforts and further improving the stabilisation fund function of Central Huijin. The ChiNext gained 11% and the STAR 50 gained 15%, so what got repriced was not earnings but the market's willingness to price in the fact that someone is holding the floor.

For business owners the implications are concrete. First, official instruments have confirmed the bottom zone, so the cushion under equity allocations just got thicker. Second, high-beta names like the STAR 50 and ChiNext are the direct beneficiaries of those instruments, but they are also the most violent, and the drawdown when sentiment cools will be fast. Do not use leverage to catch the third green candle. Third, the real winners are not the theme chasers but the companies with actual compute capacity, domestic chip orders and optical module output, and those will separate clearly once money rotates out next week.

Gold has rebounded strongly from its yearly low while US 30-year mortgage rates rose for the first time in six weeks to 7.28%, so the pricing of the rate path is genuinely unsettled.

For business owners, precious metals behave like freight rates: a sensitive line in the cost sheet, but not something to chase as a risk-off signal. If you trade metals or work in jewellery, note that spot can detach from real supply and demand during a squeeze and spreads widen on the way down. If you buy precious metal inputs, this is a good week to open the contract window and negotiate, rather than letting the market set your price.

## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **The Fed**: The federal funds target range is 3.75%-4.00%, and the final FOMC meeting of 2026 is set for Dec 8-9. After a 25 basis point hike in September, the October meeting held rates unchanged, and traders have since pared back bets on any further hike. This week the market is digesting inflation and employment data together.

**Takeaway**: This is a central bank that has already moved into pricing the tail risk of another hike. The question has shifted from when rates come down to whether they go up again, and that is the single biggest macro variable of the year. The December meeting carries far more weight than a routine one. An explicit easing signal would allow a global risk-asset repricing in the first quarter of 2027, while a continued hold leaves valuations entirely dependent on earnings delivery. If you hold dollar cash or US equity exposure, settle the pacing of your positions this week rather than reacting on Dec 9.

🏰 **The ECB**: The Oct 29 meeting held all three key rates unchanged, with the deposit facility at 2.50%, the main refinancing rate at 2.65% and the marginal lending rate at 2.90%. After hikes of 25 basis points in both June and September, the bank slowed in October, with euro area core inflation around 2.5%.

**Takeaway**: Cutting in 2024 and hiking back to 2.50% in 2026 is a regime shift, driven by inflation that proved stickier than expected. The cost environment for European demand is unfriendly, and higher euro rates keep suppressing European buyers' willingness to order. If you ship into Europe or run cross-border e-commerce, the exchange rate and the payment terms in your quotes deserve top priority in the next two months.

🇨🇳 **The PBoC**: On Nov 20 the PBoC published an LPR of 3.0% for one-year loans and 3.5% for loans over five years, unchanged for 18 consecutive months. The 7-day reverse repo rate stays at 1.4%, and a thin 1.41% net interest margin leaves pricing banks with little incentive to cut the add-on. The market now expects the easing window to slip to 2027.

**Takeaway**: The policy rate is frozen while structural easing has already been completed through market rates, with actual mortgage costs down near 2.3%. For companies this means volume is easing while price is not: banks are more willing to allocate credit to advanced manufacturing and longer-term projects, so the room to negotiate medium and long-term liquidity is wider than it was in the first half of the year. It is worth going after operating, technology and innovation loan quotas proactively.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Major Events

**US-China**: On Nov 13, China and the United States held the first meeting of the Trade Council under the bilateral economic and trade consultation mechanism via video conference, covering tariffs, the reciprocal 30-billion-for-30-billion tariff reduction arrangement, agricultural purchases and related issues. The eighth round of consultations had already taken place in New York and Washington, including the first AI dialogue under the mechanism. The latest reporting points to a sixth round of economic and trade consultations in Paris.

**Takeaway**: Institutionalised dialogue is real structural progress, because it means both sides are using a fixed channel to let pressure off. But do not read "consultations" as "orders are back". The tariff relief is reciprocal and itemised, not a blanket removal. What to watch is when the specific product lists are published, and until they are, neither shipment cadence nor payment terms should be relaxed.

**China-EU**: The Beijing meeting on Oct 9 produced 16 consensus points, with the hybrid vehicle trade understanding and a fast-track approval channel for rare earth exports called by the EU side a key step toward rebalancing trade. Yet the EU is still preparing new tariffs on Chinese goods. In late October the EU removed 84.2% of the countervailing duties on Chinese solar glass under the new rules and will refund duties collected during the review period without interest. China has initiated WTO dispute proceedings over multiple cases including EV tariffs and steel safeguard measures.

**Takeaway**: Talking and fighting at the same time is the current rhythm. Removing 84% of the duties opens one conditional door rather than opening the market, while fresh barriers hunt for workarounds through third-country transshipment. Anyone running a Europe line needs two plans: EU rules-of-origin compliance, plus a compliant chain through Turkey or Southeast Asia. Compliance costs are permanent, so stop expecting a single negotiation to erase them.

**Geopolitics**: Tensions around the Strait of Hormuz are escalating further. Iran says two tankers exploded in the strait and that an LPG carrier hit a mine; the Iranian navy commander has stated the strait will remain blocked, and the commander of the armed forces says the blockade will be tightened further. At the same time US forces intercepted a merchant vessel attempting to break the blockade in the Gulf of Oman and disabled it, and Iran says it shot down a US MQ-9 drone over the strait.

**Takeaway**: This is not a story of de-escalation, it is the fact that the shipping lane is still not open. The geopolitical premium in energy and freight has a long way left to run. If your business touches Middle East routes or relies on seaborne energy and raw materials, locking in fixed freight and fuel costs now is more sensible than betting on a thaw. For Europe and US lanes, keep buffer in your quotes and plan delivery schedules on the assumption that the corridor has not fully reopened.
