Today is Sunday, and Europe, the US and mainland China are all closed. Every price below is the **previous session's close (Friday, November 20)**, with FX and precious metals at Friday's last quotes.

**The conclusion first:** this is not an ordinary pullback. It is a textbook case of **the pricing variable switching from "growth" to "rates"**. NVIDIA down more than 43% in a week, gold and silver collapsing together, and Chinese growth stocks being sold off one-way all point to the same cause: **the market is repricing the scenario in which the Fed keeps hiking.**

**The one-line takeaway for operators: do not guess where the bottom is. Write "one more hike" into your cost model and shore up your weakest link.** If you carry dollar liabilities, cross-border quotes, AI capital spending or high-multiple equity, this is the moment to redo the arithmetic, not to pick up cheap chips.

## 🏛 Central banks and policy

🦅 **The Fed**: the October FOMC kept the federal funds rate at 3.75%–4.00% on a 10-2 vote (Cook and Hammack dissented in favour of a 25bp hike). September brought the first hike since 2023, at 25bp. The October minutes show **almost all participants judged that further tightening would be appropriate if inflation stayed above the 2% target**, with several noting the next meeting may need to consider more than 25bp. The next decision is December 8–9. **Take: the direction the market had been expecting has been completely reversed.** From "when will they cut" to "will they hike again" is the single most important repricing in this selloff. **For businesses with dollar revenue, cross-border procurement or dollar liabilities, widen your tolerance for FX volatility before December rather than betting on a direction.**

🏰 **The European Central Bank**: the fourth consecutive meeting left rates on hold, with the deposit rate at 2%. President Lagarde said geopolitical factors such as the Middle East conflict have pushed energy prices higher, and that euro-area inflation will remain clearly above the 2% target in the first half of 2027, with inflation returning to target around the end of next year as energy prices fall and the lagged effect of high rates washes through. **Take: the ECB is in a worse position than the Fed, because it is the victim of imported energy inflation and does not fully control energy prices.** EUR/USD sitting near 1.0968 tells you how the market has priced the euro: essentially "no path to rate cuts." **For anyone on the European line, the conversation now is about locking a rate, not about direction.**

🇨🇳 **The PBOC**: the LPR was left unchanged on November 20 for the 18th straight month. The PBOC's Q3 2026 Monetary Policy Report calls for reforming the benchmark for loan pricing, moving away from pricing mainly off the LPR toward a more diverse set of benchmarks, and guiding financial institutions to explore issuing floating-rate loans linked to DR001. **Take: the LPR is on hold, but the pricing mechanism is moving.** It means the anchor for interest-rate transmission is shifting from an administered quote to money-market funding rates, which for a real-economy operator means **your loan pricing will track the tightness of market liquidity directly, rather than tracking an administratively published number.** Most institutions expect a modest LPR cut after the December Central Economic Work Conference. **What to do now: when you negotiate credit lines with your main banks, ask how the rate is linked and how often it reprices. That matters far more than arguing over a single basis point.**

## 🌐 Diplomacy and major events

**US-China leaders' diplomacy**: from September 23 to 25, President Xi made a state visit to the United States, and the two sides reached an eight-point consensus including agreement to build a "constructive and strategic stabilizing relationship between China and the United States based on respect, fairness and reciprocity." On November 13, China and the United States held the **first meeting of the Trade Council** under the economic and trade consultation mechanism via video, discussing implementation of earlier outcomes across tariffs, the "30 billion for 30 billion" reciprocal tariff reduction arrangement, agricultural trade and other issues. **Take: the channel for communication is open, and institutionalised.** The phrase "reciprocal tariff reduction" means both sides are stepping back rather than escalating. **For operators in US-China trade, the next two months are a negotiation window. Talk now, and do not pin orders or capacity onto either direction prematurely.**

**China-EU trade**: the EU's countervailing case on Chinese electric vehicles has been settled as a "price undertaking" mechanism in place of heavy countervailing duties, while final anti-dumping rulings of 4.3%–45.3% have landed on categories such as tyres. EU trade barriers continue to tighten, and Chinese manufacturers are reshaping their export routes to Europe through third-country transshipment, with the overlap between RCEP and the EU's free trade agreement making Southeast Asia a long-term transit hub between China and Europe. **Take: the EU's playbook is clear. It does not negotiate on volume, it negotiates on a minimum price undertaking, trading compliance cost for competitive space.** That approach is fatal for pure price competition, and it is a moat for companies with brands, certification systems and compliance capability. **If you have EU business, the move now is not to find a cheaper transshipment route (that is a short-term survival tactic) but to build out origin tracing and digital origin certificates, because over the next five years that is your access threshold.**

**Geopolitics**: Iran and Oman have agreed on the geographic coordinates for new shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz, and a joint statement is in final review. Under the arrangement, shipping affairs in the strait will be jointly managed by Iran and Oman, the United States has for the first time recognised at the legal and institutional level Iran's right to levy service fees on passing commercial vessels, and a 60-day free-transit transition period has been set up. Iran had previously said the strait would not return to pre-war navigation until the United States honoured the June memorandum and lifted the blockade and sanctions on Iranian ports. **Take: this is the closest thing yet to an institutionalised resolution, but it depends on the United States actually delivering.** The 60-day free-transit window gives shipping and insurance some breathing room and caps energy prices. **If your business touches Middle East routes or energy costs, what you can do now is not to guess at the situation but to stress-test the freight and fuel clauses in your contracts.**
