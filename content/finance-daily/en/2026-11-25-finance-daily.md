**Commentary**: Both metals fell hard, and silver's drop was more than twice gold's. The mechanism is not complicated. Once the 10-year Treasury yield breaks 5%, the highest since 2007, the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets is repriced in one step. With the market betting on further Fed hikes, a steeper rate path always sells the most rate-sensitive precious metal first. Historically, a 5% single-day drop in silver marks a stage bottom rather than a continuation, but only if Treasury yields stop climbing. The direction of yields is the variable to watch.

## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve**: The current tightening cycle began on September 16, 2026, with one 25 basis point hike so far. Markets had split between "the Fed hikes again" and "the Fed pauses", and the late-October move in the 10-year yield above 5%, the highest since 2007, showed which way the balance had tipped. On the most recent non-farm payrolls day, the S&P 500 rose 0.31% while the 2-year Treasury yield, the most policy-sensitive point on the curve, added 5 basis points to 4.51%. **Commentary**: The Fed is no longer debating whether to hike, but how much and how often. For asset prices that is harder to value than an easing cycle, because the discount rate anchor for long-duration assets is moving up. If you run leveraged or asset-heavy businesses, the priority now is to re-underwrite your own cost of capital, not to hope for easier money.

🏰 **European Central Bank**: After hiking in June and September, the October 29 policy meeting chose to slow down and held all three key rates unchanged. **Commentary**: The pause after tightening supports the euro and temporarily relieves cost pressure on European companies. With EUR/USD still above 1.09, the market has accepted the arrangement for now. But Europe's energy cost and fiscal fragmentation remain unresolved, and the pause most likely ends with another modest tightening.

🇨🇳 **People's Bank of China**: On November 20 the National Interbank Funding Center, authorized by the central bank, published the November Loan Prime Rate with the 1-year and 5-year-plus tenors both unchanged, marking 18 consecutive months on hold. **Commentary**: Eighteen flat months reflect the combined reality that growth stabilization pressure is manageable, bank net interest margins remain constrained, and external constraints have eased without yet becoming a tailwind. In plain terms: the toolbox still has ammunition, but it is not being used. For operators the practical implication is that borrowing costs will not fall meaningfully in the short term, and the idea of stimulating demand through rate cuts is effectively off the table for this year. Improvement has to come from finding structural opportunities yourself.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Global Events

**US-China leadership diplomacy**: On September 23–25, 2026, Chinese President Xi Jinping paid a state visit to the United States. The two sides reached an eight-point consensus, including agreeing to build a constructive and strategic stabilizing relationship based on respect, fairness and reciprocity, and to support each other in hosting Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation events. Earlier, on July 30, Chinese trade lead He Lifeng held a video call with US Treasury Secretary Bessent and Trade Representative Greer, working to implement the consensus of the leaders' Beijing meeting and stabilize the trade relationship. **Commentary**: The sequence from leaders' meeting to lead negotiator calls is orderly. The real question now is not language but execution on the three sticking points: semiconductors, agricultural purchases, and fentanyl. Only what actually gets implemented counts as real improvement in the trade environment.

**China-Europe trade**: Of the 16 consensus items agreed in Beijing on October 9, the hybrid vehicle memorandum of understanding and the "green channel" for rare earth export approvals were called critical by EU Trade Commissioner Šefčovič for rebalancing EU-China trade. On trade remedies, the European Commission published a notice abolishing the definitive anti-dumping and countervailing duties on solar glass originating in China, effective October 22, 2026. EU customs will refund the duties collected during the review period without interest, and payments made from July 22, 2025 are eligible for refunds. At the same time, Europe moved again to impose new tariffs on Chinese goods shortly after the agreement was reached. **Commentary**: The removal of 84.2% in combined duties is a verifiable positive signal, showing that a pragmatic channel still exists inside the EU's trade policy. But between "consensus reached" and "measures implemented" sit local election cycles and industry lobbying on both sides. If you sell into Europe, treat the 16 points as the start of a negotiation, not as booked revenue.

**Geopolitics**: The Strait of Hormuz remains the global energy artery, carrying roughly 5.5 million barrels of Saudi crude per day, while Iran exports about 1.7 million barrels a day itself. On October 21 Iran said seven vessels had been set ablaze or damaged around the Strait and in the Persian Gulf, struck several tankers, and warned that continued US escort of tankers could bring attacks on US military bases in the region. Iranian state television aired footage of an underground drone production line for the first time. **Commentary**: This story connects directly to silver's -5.19% and gold's -2.32% today. The chain runs: escalating US-Iran confrontation, higher risk premium, yet safe-haven money flowing into the dollar and short-dated Treasuries while precious metals are trimmed on rising real yields. If the Strait were actually disrupted, crude and shipping freight would rally first, not gold. For operators in the physical economy, the useful action now is to check your own logistics and energy exposure rather than chase defensive assets.
