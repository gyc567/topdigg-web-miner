## 🏛 Central Banks & Policy

**🦅 Federal Reserve:**
New York Fed President Williams (permanent voter) said he leans toward supporting one more rate hike at the end of 2026 to prevent inflation from rebounding. However, the non-farm payrolls data released on Friday came in far below expectations, so the market immediately revised down its October hike expectations, and rate futures now show the probability of a hike at under 5%. Divergence within the Fed is evident and the uncertainty around the near-term policy path has risen.

**🏰 European Central Bank:**
Eurozone CPI rose 3.8% year on year in September, above market expectations, and pressure for an ECB hike heated up again. Lagarde previously said the fight against inflation is not yet over, and the market expects the probability of a 25bp ECB hike at the October meeting to exceed 60%. Europe's combination of slowing growth and stubbornly high inflation leaves policy in a bind.

**🇨🇳 People's Bank of China:**
China's September LPR was held unchanged, with the 1-year rate at 3.35% and the 5-year rate at 3.85%, in line with market expectations. The central bank maintains a balance between supporting growth and stabilizing the currency, with the CNY central parity staying near 7.1. The effect of the recent round of property market support policies remains to be seen, and the window for RRR cuts and rate cuts in Q4 is still open.

## 🌐 Diplomacy & Major Events

**China-US Leaders Diplomacy:**
Trump and Xi Jinping held a bilateral meeting during the G20 summit, becoming the most closely watched diplomatic event recently. After the meeting, both China and the US listed goods proposed for tariff cuts, covering areas such as agricultural products and semiconductor equipment, sending a signal of marginal easing in the trade relationship. However, experts caution that there is still a distance from the list to actual policy implementation, so excessive optimism is unwarranted.

**China-EU Trade:**
The EU urged the UK to raise tariffs on imported Chinese electric vehicles, showing that China-EU trade friction is spreading from the electric vehicle sector to a broader range of categories. German automakers, deeply dependent on the Chinese market, have clear disagreements about the tariff policy, making coordination inside the EU harder.

**Geopolitics:**
The strategic value of the Strait of Hormuz continues to draw attention. Iran nuclear deal talks have reached a deadlock, and geopolitical tension in the Middle East poses a potential threat to global energy transport, leaving oil prices vulnerable to sharp swings on unexpected events.
