## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve**: The Oct 28 FOMC kept the federal funds target range at 3.75%-4.00% unchanged in a 10-2 vote, extending the 25bp hike delivered in September. The statement said economic activity is strong and that employment and inflation are expanding together, but geopolitical developments bring a high degree of uncertainty. Chair Warsh explicitly rejected forward-looking guidance after the meeting and announced the Fed will reduce the number of policy meetings with voting members starting in 2027. The latest market data show non-farm payroll growth in November rebounding slightly from September but still weak, and the consensus view is that this data is not enough to change the December decision.

**Takeaway**: This is a central bank that has gone from "data dependence" to "data bondage". Without a stated path, the market guesses the next move at every meeting and volatility is systematically overpriced. The drop in oil prices effectively hands the Fed half a breath of relief: no need to hike for energy inflation in December. For companies borrowing in dollars, locking the exchange rate is more practical than betting on direction.

🏰 **European Central Bank**: The Oct 29 meeting kept the deposit facility rate at 2.50% and the main refinancing rate at 2.65% unchanged, with the next rate decision on Dec 17. The central bank warned that persistently high energy prices could push inflation higher through indirect and second-round effects while dragging on economic activity. Lagarde had already confirmed that a rate hike was discussed at the meeting, only deferred.

**Takeaway**: The most fragile corner of the global monetary market is in the eurozone. A hike is sitting on the table and the leadership is changing hands, so the policy uncertainty premium will persist. The oil collapse is a genuine positive for Europe: imported inflation pressure eases and one of the things weighing on eurozone assets is gone.

🇨🇳 **People's Bank of China**: October LPR stayed unchanged for the 17th straight month, with the one-year rate at 3.0% and the five-year-plus rate at 3.5%, both at historic lows. Q3 GDP grew 4.5% year-on-year, ahead of expectations, exports stayed strong, and in October CPI rose 0.7% year-on-year while PPI rose 5.7%, keeping the overall price level running smoothly and reducing the urgency for stepped-up easing. The central bank recently issued two tranches of central bank bills in Hong Kong totalling 60 billion yuan, a record single-issue size.

**Takeaway**: The PPI figure of +5.7% year on year is the number most likely to be overlooked in this section, because it says upstream prices are rising. CPI at only 0.7% says the consumer end has no ability to pass the cost through yet. Monetary easing is locked not because the central bank does not want it, but because neither the currency nor growth allows it. For business owners, funding costs will not fall faster in the short term; what really needs watching is where the fiscal push lands.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Major Events

**US-China leaders' diplomacy**: there is currently no new date set for a China-US leaders' meeting. The most recent round of economic and trade talks (September) reached consensus on a "$30 billion for $30 billion" reciprocal tariff reduction framework, and the two sides are still waiting for the next round of talks to verify the scope of the coverage list. On the US side, the recent policy focus has fallen more on investment screening and "America First" industrial policy, while China's position has consistently been to oppose politicizing economic and trade issues.

**Takeaway**: The negotiation mechanism works, but $30 billion for $30 billion is a rounding error in the bilateral trade book. **The genuinely actionable judgment**: if you export to the US, you should now go and check whether your HS code is on the tariff reduction list, rather than waiting for the outcome of the next round. If this round collapses, the cuts already secured in September are the biggest advantage you can defend.

**China-EU trade**: the EU's trade-defence actions against China keep accelerating. On Oct 30 it imposed anti-dumping duties on imported PET resin yarn products, and on Nov 6 it launched a safeguard investigation into PET resin chemicals, assessing whether safeguards are needed to protect EU industry from a surge in imports. On the other side, on Nov 5 the European Commission led a delegation of European medical device companies to Shanghai to open up the Chinese market.

**Takeaway**: Levying duties on one front while leading a sales delegation into the market on another — that is the EU's real position right now. It is tightening defensively while never stopping its push into growth markets. For Chinese exporters, intermediate goods such as PET and textiles are the exposed segment, and the line to watch is "safeguards", which are worse than anti-dumping because they can be manufactured out of thin air.

**Geopolitics**: on Nov 9 Trump said at the White House that the Iran war is "effectively over from a military standpoint", while telling Qatar's prime minister that he hopes to reach an agreement with Iran and is willing to give negotiations a chance, saying the odds of the US restarting military action are "fairly small". The same day, Iranian President Pezeshkian said the Supreme Leader had ordered an end to the state of "neither war nor peace" with the US, that Iran's "two-day plan" remains in effect, and that the proposal aims to restore security in the Strait of Hormuz and the Persian Gulf. The Iranian military, meanwhile, said its military doctrine is shifting from defense and deterrence toward an offensive posture and that it will not slow the build-out of military capabilities because of changes in US domestic politics. Also on Nov 9, North Korea test-fired a new high-speed torpedo weapons system in the eastern sea area, with deployment planned for early 2027.

**Takeaway**: This is the master switch for global risk appetite this week. The Iran ceasefire signal plus the crude collapse lit up Asian chip stocks today. But the Iranian military's "shift to an offensive posture" language contradicts the Supreme Leader's ceasefire statement, and contradictions like that usually do not disappear within 48 hours. **What business owners should do**: if you have Middle East customers or suppliers, pull out the delivery terms and force majeure clauses today and check them line by line; at the same time prepare a contingency plan for "oil back to $95", and do not build the budget on today's costs alone.
