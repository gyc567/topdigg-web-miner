## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve**: Chair Warsh has said the October and December meetings are "extremely important" and called AI the most profound economic transformation of his lifetime. Market-implied odds of a hike have fallen from around 27% to under 15%, and the consensus is that the Fed holds the federal funds target range at 3.75%-4.00% on October 28.
> Commentary: Falling hike odds are the main driver of this week's risk-on rally, but Warsh's hawkish streak hasn't gone anywhere. The real action isn't in October, it's in December. If inflation data refuses to cooperate, "hold" can turn into "one more hike" with little warning.

🏰 **European Central Bank**: The ECB hiked 25bp on October 16, lifting the deposit facility rate to 2.50% and main refinancing operations to 2.65%, its first hike in three years, arguing that inflation pressure has outweighed growth concerns. President Schnabel then signalled further tightening, citing euro-area resilience and energy-driven inflation risk.
> Commentary: The ECB has turned hawkish, yet the euro hasn't gained ground, because growth worries and the US-Europe rate differential are pulling the other way. This round of tightening hurts European equities and peripheral bonds more than it helps the currency.

🇨🇳 **People's Bank of China**: The LPR has been unchanged for 16 straight months, with the 1-year rate at 3.0% and the 5-year-plus rate at 3.5%. PBOC-linked media commentary argues that "the need to cut the policy rate directly is not high," with policy leaning toward structural tools and targeted support.
> Commentary: Neither prices nor property data create urgency to cut, and with the yuan relatively strong at 6.67, an outright cut would add FX pressure. Growth stabilisation leans more on fiscal policy, and the workload of physical projects is rising.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Major Events

**US-China leadership diplomacy**: The trade truce has been extended by two months, with both sides publishing a "30 billion for 30 billion" reciprocal tariff-cut framework covering roughly $60 billion in goods. The truce expiry is the key date; until then both sides likely keep talking without tearing each other apart.
> Commentary: Extending the truce buys breathing room, it doesn't solve anything. The real watershed comes after expiry, and supply-chain de-risking is already irreversible.

**China-EU trade**: On October 12, China and the EU reached 16 points of consensus, with plug-in hybrid exports to be halved and a tariff war averted. At the same time China launched an anti-dumping investigation against the EU, and Brussels is weighing a trade-control "kill switch" that could exclude Chinese companies from its market in the event of trade imbalances.
> Commentary: Autos are the one issue neither side can afford to escalate, so quotas and consensus are being used to defuse it. But the EU is leaning on trade defence instruments more often, so friction will spread from autos into agriculture and more manufacturing.

**Geopolitics**: Iran's nuclear file remains the biggest variable. Iranian officials say all enriched material will stay in the country, Washington says the immediate focus is the Strait of Hormuz, and Trump has even floated strikes on the Fordow facility. Iran appears willing to drag talks past the US midterms. A Russia-Ukraine "energy ceasefire" has failed, and Washington has redirected its pressure toward Tehran.
> Commentary: Classic trading of time, with each side waiting for a more favourable clock. If Hormuz goes wrong, oil and gold jump together and central banks instantly lose room to ease. That is the risk most under-priced in every asset right now.
