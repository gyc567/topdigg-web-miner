## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **The Fed**: The federal funds target range is 3.75%-4.00%, and the last FOMC meeting of 2026 is set for Dec 8-9. Markets are stuck in a neither-cut-nor-hike stance, waiting on this week's inflation and employment data.

**Takeaway**: The final meeting of the year carries more weight than any other. An explicit easing signal would open room for a global risk-asset re-rating in Q1; continued hold leaves valuations dependent on earnings. If you hold US equities or global assets, settle the pace of your dollar cash positions this week instead of reacting on Dec 9.

🏰 **The ECB**: The Oct 29 meeting held all three key rates unchanged, with the deposit facility at 2.50%, the main refinancing rate at 2.65% and the marginal lending rate at 2.90%, in line with expectations. The bank had hiked 25 basis points in both June and September before slowing in October, with euro area core inflation around 2.5%.

**Takeaway**: Cutting in 2024 then hiking back to 2.50% in 2026 is a regime shift driven by stickier inflation than expected. For anyone exporting to Europe, the price environment on the demand side is not friendly, and rising euro rates keep squeezing European buyers' willingness to order.

🇨🇳 **The PBoC**: The loan prime rate has been on hold for months, at 3% for one-year and 3.5% for loans over five years. Institutions broadly expect one or two more cuts this year, delivered mainly through open market operations and reserve requirement ratio cuts, which are quantity-based tools.

**Takeaway**: The benchmark rate is frozen, yet actual mortgage costs have fallen to around 2.3%. Structural easing is completing itself through market rates. For business owners the more practical point is that banks need RRR relief for medium and long-term liquidity, which changes the room you have to negotiate loans. That is a conversation worth having proactively in the second half of this year.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Major Events

**US-China**: On Nov 13, China and the United States held the first meeting of the Trade Council under the bilateral economic and trade consultation mechanism via video conference, covering tariffs, the reciprocal 30-billion-for-30-billion tariff reduction arrangement, agricultural purchases and related issues. The eighth round of consultations had already taken place in New York and Washington from Sept 20-23, including the first AI dialogue under the mechanism.

**Takeaway**: Institutionalized dialogue means both sides are building a structural channel to defuse tension. For cross-border business this is good news but not a free pass. Tariff relief is reciprocal and itemized, not a blanket removal. Do not read negotiation headlines as a recovery in orders; watch for when the specific product lists are published.

**China-EU**: A Beijing meeting on Oct 9 produced 16 consensus points, with the hybrid vehicle trade understanding and a fast-track approval channel for rare earth exports called by the EU side a key step toward rebalancing trade. Yet after those agreements, Europe moved again to prepare new tariffs on Chinese goods. At the same time, in late October the EU removed 84.2% of the countervailing duties on Chinese solar glass meeting the new rules, and will refund duties collected during the review period without interest. China has initiated WTO dispute proceedings over multiple cases including EV tariffs and steel safeguard measures.

**Takeaway**: Talking and fighting at the same time is the current China-EU rhythm. An 80% tariff removal is a targeted door opening, not full liberalization, and new barriers keep hunting for workarounds through third-country transshipment. Anyone running a Europe line needs two plans ready: EU rules-of-origin compliance, plus a compliant chain through Turkey or Southeast Asia. Compliance costs are permanent, so stop expecting a single negotiation to erase them.

**Geopolitics**: The US-Iran conflict continues, and transit through the Strait of Hormuz remains far below pre-conflict levels. According to Reuters and Statista data, daily vessel traffic had fallen to roughly 10 by mid-July, against about 25 in late June and around 100 at the start of the year. Mediated by Qatar, Pakistan and others, the US-Iran talks have gone through several rounds, and the framework for restoring navigation has not fully collapsed.

**Takeaway**: The geopolitical premium in energy prices and shipping costs has not run its course. If you import energy or run logistics through Middle East routes, locking in a fixed freight and fuel cost beats betting on de-escalation. Cross-border sellers should also note that any renewed tightening would immediately push up freight rates and delivery times on Europe and US lanes, so leave buffer in your quotes.
