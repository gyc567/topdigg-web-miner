Friday (Nov 20) was not an ordinary pullback. Three fuses lit at the same time: NVIDIA came under an SEC accounting probe, the Nasdaq lost 5.28% in a single session, and gold shed nearly 9% in a week. Meanwhile in China, the Shanghai Composite printed its longest losing streak of the year, seven sessions in a row, with 5,236 stocks falling.

**The conclusion first:** this is not "the AI story is over." It is a **repricing of the risk premium**. Rate-hike expectations (a 25bp December hike now priced at 92.7%), Democrats taking both chambers of Congress after the midterms (which raises the odds of AI regulation), and a single-name regulatory shock (the SEC probe into NVIDIA's accounting) all landed together and pulled the valuation anchor of every long-duration asset lower in one move.

**One line for founders:** this is a signal to re-lever your risk, not to bottom-fish. If your business carries dollar costs, AI capital expenditure, or high-multiple equity, the job now is to shore up the weakest link, not to pick up what looks cheap.

## 📈 US Equities

**The Nov 20 session:** the Nasdaq Composite fell **5.28%**, the S&P 500 **3.70%**, the Dow **1.93%**, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index **10.73%**. All three major indices entered correction territory.

🍎 **Apple (AAPL)** closed at **$342.56** (-1.90%), market cap **$4.98T**, P/E (TTM) **38.15**, forward P/E 36.37, analyst target **$345.63** (just +0.9% above the last price), 52-week range 243.42–360.83, beta 1.09, RSI(14) **46.61**.

**Take:** **a price target essentially equal to the current price means the sell side has run out of valuation to offer.** The business itself is intact: revenue $478.8B (+15.1%), net income $132.2B (+18.0%), EPS $8.98 (+20.4%). **What broke is the valuation anchor.** Paying 38 times earnings for a business compounding at 20% has to compress when the discount rate rises, and on Friday the market shed 3.7% while Apple fell only 1.9%. **That relative resilience says the market has already reclassified Apple from an offensive asset into a defensive one.** So for holders: the value in this name now sits in cash flow and dividends, not in imagination. Do not expect it to outperform in the next rate shock.

🚗 **Tesla (TSLA)** closed at **$345.82** (-7.64%), market cap **$1.39T**, P/E (TTM) **385.92**, forward P/E 164.45, analyst target **$417.53** (+18.87%), 52-week range 297.38–498.83, beta 1.74, RSI(14) **37.72**.

**Take:** **the 7.64% one-day drop came from three overlapping causes: beta drag from the AI complex selling off, a rejection of the FSD regulatory exemption in Korea, and dilution concerns around a potential SpaceX-Tesla merger** (one commentator warned the merger could erase roughly 24% of the premium within weeks). But the number that should stop you is this: **385 times earnings attached to an income statement with net profit down 36.7% year on year.**

**In other words, there is zero earnings in today's Tesla price. Every dollar of it is expectation.** What makes it worse is that **with Democrats now controlling both chambers after the midterms, Elon Musk's policy risk has migrated from reputational risk to legislative risk**, and the regulatory environment for autonomous driving is tightening. **My call for founders: Tesla in this rate regime is a high-volatility trading instrument, not an allocation asset. If you own it at 385x earnings, position sizing matters more than direction.**

🔥 **NVIDIA (NVDA)** closed at **$125.53** (-14.01%), market cap **$3.03T** (more than 31% below its yearly high), P/E (TTM) **13.47**, forward P/E 8.64, analyst target **$244.18** (+94.77%), 52-week range 125.37–243.37, beta 2.25, RSI(14) **15.25**.

**Take:** **this is the single most counterintuitive data combination of the session and it deserves the most attention.**

On Friday afternoon, Reuters reported that **SEC enforcement staff are examining whether NVIDIA has potential accounting issues**. The Wall Street Journal had reported the existence of the investigation on Nov 17 but said the focus could not be determined. Shares, already down roughly 7%, widened to a 13.7%–14% loss, **destroying about $400B of market value in one session** and setting a seventh consecutive daily decline along with a new 52-week low. AMD and Micron also fell more than 13%, which tells you this was **not a single-stock event but a systemic liquidation of the entire semiconductor complex**.

**Now look at this combination: revenue $361.8B (+93.3%), net income $226.3B (+128.1%), EPS $9.31 (+130.4%), a P/E of just 13.47 and a forward P/E of 8.64.** The earnings are the strongest in the group, the valuation is the cheapest in the group, and the stock performance is the worst in the group. **The cause is not fundamental. It sits in two places: a regulatory trust discount (once the SEC starts asking, the "printing press of the AI era" narrative immediately gains a question mark), and the systematic pressure a rising discount rate puts on long-duration assets. With a beta of 2.25, a 1% market decline becomes a 2.25% decline here.**

**RSI at 15.25 is textbook oversold, and a $244 target implies 95% of upside remains.** Those two facts coexist, and together they describe the actual trade structure right now: **oversold plus cheap plus a regulatory shadow. That is a short-term bounce candidate, not a "bottoming is complete" signal for the medium term.** And remember the SEC has not reached a conclusion, so this discount does not evaporate on its own. **If your portfolio is overweight AI, the right move today is not to buy the dip. It is to convert position sizing from belief into discipline: build a plan around a 13x earnings price, and only then decide whether you want to add anything into the panic.**

## 🥇 🥈 Commodities

**Gold:** spot **$3,539.14/oz** (-5.38%), with an intraday high of 3626.74 and low of 3502.70, closing near $3,537.87. **The week brought a loss of nearly 9%**, and the $3,500 level is now being tested.

**Silver:** spot **$41.25/oz** (-9.32%), intraday high 43.10, low 40.76.

**Take:** **gold down 9% in a week and silver down 9% in a day, while geopolitical risk is still elevated, is itself the most important signal of the session.** For the past year, "geopolitical hedging" was the pillar holding up gold. That pillar has now broken, and **the dominant pricing variable has switched from safe-haven sentiment to real interest rates.** The logic is simple: a 25bp hike raises the opportunity cost of holding a zero-yield asset, and every non-earning asset gets drained at the same time.

**Silver fell harder than gold because it is also half an industrial metal**, taking manufacturing demand weakness on top of the opportunity cost. Domestic prices followed: gold was quoted at 765.51 yuan per gram on Nov 20, down 27.18 yuan (-3.43%), with silver at 9.53 yuan per gram.

**Here is the practical advice for operators: if you are in energy or raw-material cost-sensitive industries (chemicals, logistics, manufacturing, cross-border e-commerce freight), run an "oil +25%, freight +15%" stress test now.** The exercise costs an hour. Getting it wrong once can cost millions in profit.

**The other thing worth doing:** if you have been planning to allocate to precious metals, **the window for scaling in is right here, not "after it falls further."** The reason is simple: the slope of the hiking cycle is known (another 25bp in December is highly likely), but **once the hike lands, the pressure of real rates on gold is released in one go, not day by day.** Waiting for "complete capitulation" at a price where 90% of the bad news is already priced usually means waiting forever.

## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve:** the federal funds target range remains at **3.75%–4.00%**. The October FOMC minutes showed "almost all participants" judged a further increase in the target range likely appropriate, and Waller publicly suggested the central bank might need 50bp to "shock people." **Market data on Nov 19 put the probability of a 25bp December hike at 92.7%.** The October nonfarm payrolls report on Nov 6 showed 165,000 jobs added, a large beat, which is where this round of hike expectations reignited.

**Take:** **the swing from "rate-cut trade" to "three consecutive hikes" is a complete narrative reversal, and the market needed only days to complete it.** What matters more is the **nature** of Friday's equity decline: it was not "one company had bad news," it was the tightening of three ropes at once, on rate expectations, regulatory expectations and the election result, all pulling on long-duration assets simultaneously.

**For founders: your dollar cost of capital, your dollar asset valuations, and global risk appetite over the next 12 months all need to be recalculated on this new anchor.** In concrete terms, **do not make any investment decision that depends on dollar rates being certain before the December 9 meeting. For cross-border e-commerce and AI infrastructure projects with heavy dollar inputs, write "one more hike" into the funding cost model.**

🏰 **European Central Bank:** on October 29 the ECB Governing Council **left its three key rates unchanged**, slowing its pace after hikes in June and September. At the same time, reporting indicates **the ECB will discuss raising the reserve rate at next week's meeting**, with ING describing the move as "bringing financing pressure forward." EUR/USD has fallen to a 19-month low while eurozone inflation has risen to 4%.

**Take:** **4% inflation paired with a 19-month low exchange rate, and the central bank is still discussing tightening, tells you the ECB's inflation pressure is coming mainly from the supply and energy side, and they have chosen to respond by tightening while they still can.** For anyone selling into Europe: **eurozone asset valuations (property, cyclicals, European banks) need to come down, the marginal cost of European demand is rising, and the window for quoting is narrowing.** More usefully: if European customers are still squeezing your price, **this is the moment to hold your ground politely, because their position is tighter than yours.**

🇨🇳 **People's Bank of China:** the 1-year LPR stays at 3.0% and the 5-year-plus LPR at 3.5%, **unchanged for 18 consecutive months**. The PBOC's latest Q3 2026 Monetary Policy Implementation Report sets out a plan to **advance interest-rate pricing benchmark reform, moving from pricing primarily off the LPR toward a more diversified set of benchmarks**, and to guide financial institutions in exploring floating-rate loans linked to DR001. The 2026 policy stance is "appropriately accommodative" and "flexible and efficient in using reserve requirement cuts and rate cuts."

**Take:** **18 months of a frozen LPR does not mean policy inaction. The action is happening at the pricing mechanism layer.** As DR001-linked floating-rate lending spreads, **bank quotes will track money-market rates more often, and your window to lock a fixed rate is narrowing and becoming less reliable.** **Practical advice: go ask your bank for a DR001-linked product while simultaneously locking one fixed-rate loan. Run both, and pin down your cost range.** Do not wait for "the rate cut to land." With the Fed hiking consecutively, domestic rates are most likely to stay put, bank net interest margins are already thin, and **there will be no surprise relief on your cost of capital. Do not build your expansion plan around "waiting for a cut."**

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Major Events

**US-China leaders' diplomacy:** on September 24, 2026, President Xi held talks with President Trump at the White House, and the two sides agreed to build a "constructive and strategic stabilizing relationship between China and the United States based on respect, fairness and reciprocity," reaching eight points of consensus. **On November 13, the Trade Council under the China-US economic and trade consultation mechanism held its first meeting**, discussing tariffs, a "30 billion for 30 billion" reciprocal tariff reduction arrangement, agricultural trade and other topics. Trump arrived in Beijing on Nov 18 to open trade talks and the leaders' meeting. **However, reporting on Nov 20 shows the talks still leave open questions on agricultural procurement.**

**Take:** **the cadence is "talk first, sign later, leave gaps," not "one package lands."** Agricultural procurement matters precisely because it is **the easiest thing to quantify, the easiest thing to deliver and the most visible to voters.** **One honest line for exporters: whatever actually lands on paper will most likely be concentrated in consumer goods and agriculture. Do not budget high-end equipment and semiconductor-related lines at zero tariff before the first half of 2027.** And do not miss a variable that just changed: **with Democrats now controlling both chambers of Congress, the political momentum for quickly ratifying a China-US trade agreement has actually declined**, so the timetable may run longer than markets expect.

**China-EU trade:** the latest round of the Trade and Investment Council talks (TIC) produced **16 points of consensus**. At the same time, EU measures toward China are splitting apart: **effective October 22, the EU repealed the definitive anti-dumping and countervailing duties on Chinese-origin solar glass (a combined 84.2%), and qualifying importers may reclaim duties already paid, without interest**. On steel, however, the EU keeps tightening, levying anti-dumping duties on certain cold-rolled flat steel and on Vietnamese steel.

**Take:** **the EU's posture is selective easing and targeted strike.** It is easing on the green supply chain where China is competitive and American companies need the input too, and striking at traditional capacity like steel. **That is not a signal of improving relations; it is a signal of structural bifurcation.** For cross-border operators, **if a competitor of yours in the EU is being hit while your product category happens to sit on the list being eased, that is a window you can go negotiate this week.** Concretely: check the current EU duty rate on your HS codes and confirm whether your Vietnam or Southeast Asian suppliers have been added to any tariff list.

**Geopolitics:** on June 18, 2026, Pakistan's Prime Minister Sharif confirmed that Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz with the United States lifting its blockade at the same time, under the US-Iran Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, ending a near 110-day full blockade and restoring commercial shipping, though **the first high-level round in Bürgenstock, Switzerland had collapsed**. On November 19, Trump, Xi and Putin held a 90-minute tripartite meeting in Beijing and issued a "joint peace statement," but **the statement did not mention the war in Ukraine or Iran**.

**Take:** **the value of that joint statement is not in its content, it is in the fact of the three sitting in the same frame.** Leaving out Ukraine and Iran means **these three fronts were deliberately left blank, reserved as future bargaining chips rather than already settled.**

**And here is the most practical point for operators: because nuclear risk is unresolved, the energy risk premium still sits in oil, not in gold.** It is striking that gold collapsed at the same time, because **the market has temporarily chosen to believe that rate hikes beat safe-haven demand.** But if the Hormuz talks genuinely break down and a real blockade emerges, oil becomes the final straw that breaks inflation, pushing the Fed even further hawkish, and only then does the safe-haven bid in precious metals come back to life. **So today, holding "long oil" alongside "short gold" is internally consistent. Holding "long gold" alongside "long growth stocks" is not.**
