## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve**: October CPI rose 0.4% m/m against consensus of 0.3%, and the inflation print is broadening. Fed Governor Waller publicly said December may require a 50bp hike, with multiple officials pushing the hawkish case. CME FedWatch now shows a 95.6% probability of a 25bp hike at the December 9 meeting. The federal funds target range is currently 3.75%–4.00%. Treasury yields recorded their biggest rise since the tariff crisis.

*Takeaway*: This is the turning point of the cycle. Markets had priced a "hiking tail" and that has now been rewritten to "one more, maybe two more." **What it means for operators**: the valuation anchor for global assets is moving up. Funding costs, M&A prices and startup valuations are all getting squeezed. **What to do**: If you run a financing-dependent business or cross-border arbitrage, re-run your December cost of capital with a 50bp hike. Do not make decisions off last quarter's model.

🏰 **ECB**: The Governing Council held all three policy rates on October 29, deposit facility at 2.50%, main refinancing operations at 2.65% and marginal lending at 2.90%, after a 25bp hike in September. The October statement flagged rising energy prices as an upside risk to the inflation outlook.

*Takeaway*: The ECB is hiking passively, pushed by energy costs rather than tightening deliberately. The euro area now has European equities near record highs against stressed European bonds and a pressured euro. That is a systemic repricing of European asset beta.

🇨🇳 **PBOC**: LPR has been unchanged for 16 consecutive months, with both the one-year and five-year rates untouched. Goldman Sachs pushed its forecast for the "double easing" (10bp policy rate cut plus 50bp RRR cut) from Q4 2025 to sometime after now. Offshore CNH is flat around 6.676, with ample room for exchange-rate autonomy.

*Takeaway**: The China-US rate gap is widening, yet CNY is not depreciating, which means the PBOC chose stability over defending the currency. Resilient exports and a modest CPI/PPI recovery give policymakers a reason not to reach for tools yet. **What to do**: Stable onshore funding costs means the LPR window is still open, but do not expect it to become an equity liquidity story.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Major Events

**US-China**: Real-time search was restricted for this round, so this section is compiled from recent publicly available information. The confirmed main line of US-China interaction still centers on tariff and supply chain negotiations, moving at a visibly slower pace than the previous high-intensity phase. Beijing keeps the "settle differences through negotiation" framing at multilateral venues, with the negotiating chips concentrated on three core issues: tariff rates, technology restrictions and supply chain access. Worth flagging: with the Fed accelerating its hiking cycle, both sides will take more cautious negotiating postures, so the odds of a "major breakthrough" in the near term are low. **What it means for operators**: do not treat US-China relations as a single tradable variable. Every link in your export chain, chip chain and consumer chain needs its own Plan B.

**China-EU trade**: In October the EU announced the removal of two trade remedy duties on specific Chinese goods, with combined rates of up to 84.2%, plus refunds of duties collected during the review period, interest excluded, applicable to payments made from July 22, 2025. That is a rare easing in China-EU friction. But the direction is simultaneously shifting: on November 6 China's Ministry of Commerce published two case-opening notices at once, launching an anti-dumping investigation into imported para-aramid filament from the EU, and an anti-dumping case on imported ethylene norbornene from the EU and Japan. **Our read**: the main battlefield is migrating from "star tracks" like autos and batteries toward chemical fibre and chemical materials, the unglamorous but essential categories. China-EU trade combat has entered a tit-for-tat round format. **What to do**: for chemical fibre, chemical and building materials exporters into Europe, do two things now. Map your anti-dumping duty exposure SKU by SKU, and make your third-country transshipment routes properly compliant. Transshipment can be legal, but origin-washing under EU anti-circumvention investigations carries extreme risk. Do not chase small savings here.

**Geopolitics**: Overnight, Houthi forces struck Saudi financial and oil facilities, and three tankers exploded in the Strait of Hormuz. At the same time crude extended its decline as the market bet on further de-escalation in Iran, with Brent briefly under $90. **Our read**: classic "the event is still live, the price moves first." A tanker explosion is inherently a premium signal, but the market chose to trade de-escalation, which means money in the market does not believe this escalates into a sustained supply cut. **What to do**: near-term energy and shipping costs look like a relief window. You have the leverage right now in procurement talks for fuel, bitumen and chemical feedstocks. Do not wait until December to sign.
