## 📈 US Equities Focus

🍎 **Apple (AAPL)**: $356.27 (+0.39%, after hours $355.40, -0.24%), market cap $5.18 trillion, P/E (TTM) 39.67, forward P/E 37.89, analyst consensus target $344.62 (-3.27% vs spot), range $250 to $425, RSI(14) 60.6, 52-week range $243.42 - $360.83.

Commentary: On a day NVDA fell 2%, AAPL still closed higher, which means the market is rotating rather than fleeing tech. It is not abandoning technology, it is abandoning the most fragile slice of the AI trade. The consensus target still sits 3.27% below the current price, meaning the sell side's valuation anchor has not caught up with the stock. That is not "expensive," it is "consensus lagging." The real pressure sits on the cost side: memory price inflation is eating into iPhone gross margin, and the question for 2027 is whether AI hardware costs can be passed through.

🚗 **Tesla (TSLA)**: $390.22 (-1.82%, after hours $387.19, -0.78%), market cap $1.54 trillion, P/E (TTM) 428.73, forward P/E 182.53, analyst consensus target $412.48 (+5.70%), range $128.11 to $600, RSI(14) 53.5, 52-week range $297.38 - $498.83.

Commentary: A 428x multiple alongside a price that has retraced nearly 22% from $498.83 tells you the market has never resolved the question of what Tesla actually is. Among 44 bullish analysts, the average target is only 5.7% above spot, while the range spans 128 to 600. This price level does not mean cheap, it means no consensus valuation. The decision rule is simple: do not use TSLA to express any opinion about AI or EVs. Its volatility right now comes from narrative, not fundamentals.

🔥 **NVIDIA (NVDA)**: $162.85 (-2.01%, after hours $160.88, -1.21%), market cap $3.93 trillion, P/E (TTM) 20.59, forward P/E 13.48, analyst consensus target $283.31 (+74.0% vs spot, 61 institutions rated "buy"), RSI(14) 24.9, 52-week range $148.80 - $243.37.

Commentary: This is the single line in the whole piece that deserves serious attention. RSI 24.9 is textbook oversold, and yet the consensus target sits 74% above the current price. Both being true simultaneously means the market is in violent disagreement, not in unanimous bearishness. The disagreement is not about valuation, it is about governance: after Huang's departure, what the market fears was never "AI demand is gone," it was "can the successor sustain the same capital expenditure pace and the same policy access." A 20.59x P/E is not expensive for a company still growing earnings at double digits; the problem is that this is the highest-liquidity-premium stock in the market, so any governance noise gets amplified into a valuation discount. November 17 after the close is the earnings report, and that is the hardest observation window coming up. The position guidance is blunt: do not use heavy size in the oversold zone to bet on a bounce. Use a small position, wait for earnings, and add if you are right.

## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve**: The October meeting held the federal funds rate at 3.75% - 4.00%. The prior 25bp hike in September was the first increase since 2023. The statement says economic activity continues to expand strongly and inflation remains somewhat elevated, and two members voted to hike by 25 basis points. Chair Warsh announced that starting in 2027 the number of rate meetings requiring a vote will be reduced. The next FOMC meeting is December 8-9, the last of the year.

Commentary: This is the textbook "I still want to tighten but I am afraid of hitting employment" state, and that is exactly what makes the market uncomfortable. Two votes for a hike means December cut expectations can basically be cleared out, but there is not enough evidence for a hike either. In other words, the marginal direction of global liquidity over the next two quarters is most likely flat or slightly tighter. For anyone holding AI hardware, compute, or long-duration assets, the cash cost over the next two quarters matters far more than the valuation story. The reduction in voting meetings for 2027 is also an underrated signal: slower policy reaction means bigger volatility, so do not expect the Fed to smooth over every shock for you.

🏰 **European Central Bank**: The October meeting held rates unchanged, with the main refinancing rate at 2.65% and the deposit facility rate at 2.50%. The ECB warned that high energy prices could push inflation higher, while underlying inflation remains broadly stable and wage pressures show no obvious response. Lagarde confirmed at the post-meeting press conference that the Governing Council discussed a hike but agreed unanimously to hold. There are also reports that Lagarde plans to leave early.

Commentary: Europe's problem was never policy, it is growth and energy costs. Lagarde's potential early departure on top of French bond volatility is a more important variable than the rate decision itself: during a leadership gap at the ECB, the market will price the euro more emotionally. EUR/USD breaking below 1.105 already makes the point. The judgment for business owners is direct: Europe is not your growth market right now, at least not that market. Your growth is in Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Latin America, and the part of domestic demand being displaced. To judge whether a market is worth entering, look at three things: demand growth, market access barriers, and whether you have an irreplaceable position. Europe scores zero on all three.

🇨🇳 **People's Bank of China**: The October LPR was held steady for the 17th consecutive month, with the 1-year rate at 3.0% and the 5-year-plus rate at 3.5%, both at historic lows. Third quarter GDP grew 4.5%, better than expected. Governor Pan Gongsheng said the central bank will push forward with a reform of the pricing benchmark, reducing the excessive reliance on LPR alone, and the market read this as a signal that the central bank may shift toward using the 7-day reverse repo rate as the main policy rate.

Commentary: Better-than-expected data gives the central bank a reason not to hurry. But "better than expected" is itself a trap, because it keeps lowering the urgency of additional stimulus and therefore suppresses cyclical elasticity. What actually deserves attention is the pricing benchmark reform: LPR fading and the 7-day reverse repo moving up means the interest rate transmission chain is being rearranged, and every business that prices loans off LPR needs to rebuild its cost of capital model. The next LPR quote date is November 20.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Major Events

**China-US leaders and trade**: SCMP reports that Xi Jinping and Trump plan to meet next week, with trade negotiations expected to stay on track. China's October import growth exceeded exports, pushing the trade surplus past $1 trillion. At the same time, SCMP cited analysts saying that if the US escalates chip controls over the Nvidia case, China may restrict rare earth exports. Separately, there are reports that a Chinese company procured banned Nvidia chips and installed them in a data center in Shanghai. The US Senate Banking Committee has requested that Nvidia and Commerce Secretary Lutnick appear for testimony.

Commentary: Connect these three and the logic becomes obvious. Nvidia is not just a company, it is the policy interface through which the US controls compute flowing to China. When the person at that interface changes, the rules of the interface get rewritten. Next week's China-US meeting is the window for that repricing. For owners in Chinese AI compute, chips, servers, magnet materials, or rare earths, this is not sentiment, it is order structure: the window for domestic substitution opens while uncertainty on imported compute rises in parallel. Do not read this as simply good or bad. This is a supply chain repricing. The opportunity sits on the domestic side with dependable delivery capability, the risk sits with businesses betting everything on a single import channel.

**China-Europe trade**: Chinese exports are hitting new barriers in Southeast Asia, with Thailand imposing a 30% tariff on Chinese e-commerce sellers and cross-border sellers already calculating the impact. In Europe, BMW's Shenyang joint venture base announced large-scale layoffs because of intense competition from Chinese domestic automakers, hitting the local industrial economy. A Dutch court ordered the destruction of seized documents in the BYD versus Jaguar Land Rover intellectual property case. Meanwhile the China-Europe contest over rare earths and industrial security keeps heating up.

Commentary: These three cases point at the same thing: Chinese manufacturing going abroad is shifting from "tariffs" to the triple pressure of "compliance barriers + local competition + intellectual property lawsuits." Thailand's 30% tariff is only the outermost layer. The real cost of overseas markets also includes local production requirements, compressed channel margins, and IP lawsuits that can be filed at any time. The judgment for business owners: if your overseas model still assumes "make it well at home, sell abroad, earn on low price," that model does not hold after 2026. There are only two exits: either move profit into local production and services, or build a product with brand premium that others cannot route around.

**Geopolitics**: In the Middle East, the IAEA has asked Iran to reopen nuclear facilities for inspections and said it cannot verify whether the country's underground enrichment site holds nuclear material. Debate continues in Israel over whether to strike Iran before the current government leaves office. On the same day, reports of Iran-war developments appeared alongside reports of Houthi attacks on Saudi energy and military facilities, with Trump previously saying the US does not need to reach a deal with Iran. On the Russia-Ukraine front, both sides reported overnight attacks and US efforts to broker a ceasefire made limited progress; a car bomb attack targeting figures linked to Ukraine in Russia killed an officer and wounded a former Ukrainian prime minister's adviser.

Commentary: The geopolitical premium in oil and the safe-haven premium in gold are both still there, but note that gold has been falling for two days, which means money is not choosing "buy precious metals to hide" this time, it is choosing "stay in cash and dollars." That is the market's real attitude toward geopolitical risk right now: acknowledge the risk, refuse to pay a premium for it. For business owners with real operations, Middle East uncertainty transmits straight into freight, insurance, and delivery cycles. Price that layer in when you quote, and do not accept orders using pre-war costs.

**Also worth watching**: China's October import growth exceeded exports and the trade surplus passed $1 trillion, a structure worth tracking on its own (it means domestic demand for imports is recovering, and it also means the bargaining chip in trade friction is growing); Thailand imposed a 30% tariff on Chinese cross-border e-commerce goods; at the Nvidia board level, "mismatched composition" is widely questioned, and multiple institutional investors do not believe the succession issue can be resolved within a few months.
