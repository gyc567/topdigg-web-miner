## 📊 Forex Market

- **EUR/USD**: 1.09716 (+0.09%), intraday range 1.09690-1.09745. The euro held a narrow band around 1.0970, with volatility under 5 basis points.
- **USD/JPY**: 159.09 (-0.11%), range 159.066-159.228. The dollar consolidated sideways against the yen.
- **USD/CNH (offshore renminbi)**: 6.6661 (+0.01%), range 6.66258-6.66598. The offshore renminbi barely moved, with an amplitude below 4 basis points.

All three major currency pairs entered narrow ranges at the same time. That says the market is waiting for a directional catalyst rather than actively pricing one.

On the domestic front, Chinese A-shares fell on heavier turnover on November 25. The Shanghai Composite closed at 3476.47, down 2.03%; the Shenzhen Component closed at 10086.36, down 3.24%; the ChiNext Index closed at 2183.58, down 4.19%; the STAR 50 closed at 1186.14, down 5.12%; and the CSI 300 closed at 3898.24, down 1.65%. Turnover across the two exchanges came to RMB 1009.9 billion, an increase of RMB 220.2 billion, with only 586 stocks advancing and 4926 declining. Main-fund net outflows ran to roughly RMB 49 billion. The worst damage landed on the small-cap end: the Beijing Stock Exchange 50 fell 4.75%, the CSI 1000 fell 4.44%, and the Guozheng 2000 fell 4.43%. Money rotated into dividend and agricultural names, with banks, baijiu and insurers leading inflows, while semiconductors and electronic chemicals lagged.

**Commentary**: This was a day where the smaller the name, the harder it fell, which means the selling pressure came from valuation and liquidity preference rather than from the economic fundamentals. The fact that dividend payers were defended tells you the market was on defense, not on offense. For founders, the priority in this kind of shrinking-turnover selloff is not chasing hot themes, it is running the numbers on your own cash flow and leverage. When the market broadens lower, staying solvent matters more than participating.

## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **The Federal Reserve**: The current federal funds rate target range is 3.75%-4.00%. The minutes of the October meeting were released on November 18, and the next rate decision lands on December 8-9. Since those minutes, market pricing has shown no clear one-way tilt, and the dollar index has flattened out.

**Commentary**: The December meeting is the last major window of the year. Until the Fed speaks, neither the dollar nor gold has a directional engine, which explains today's narrow ranges in both FX and precious metals. If you run cross-border flows, do not bet this month's settlement window on a single direction. Arranging hedges will serve you better than guessing the level.

🏰 **The European Central Bank**: On September 10 the ECB raised its three key rates by 25 basis points each, taking the deposit facility rate to 2.50%, the main refinancing rate to 2.65% and the marginal lending rate to 2.90%, with the new rates effective September 16. This was the ECB's second hike in 2026, justified by energy price inflation from the Middle East conflict, with euro area inflation rising from 2.9% in July to 3.3% in August, of which energy was up 14.3% year on year. The ECB forecasts 2026 euro area inflation at 3% and 2027 at 2.5%, with growth of 0.9% and 1.4%.

**Commentary**: Europe has switched from loose recovery to inflation defense. Energy-driven inflation is dangerous precisely because it squeezes corporate margins without creating room to cut rates, so the cost of doing business in Europe stays structurally expensive. If your business touches the European market, build your 2027 pricing now under a high-rate assumption, not after the costs have already passed through.

🇨🇳 **The People's Bank of China**: On November 20 the PBOC published November loan prime rates, with the one-year rate at 3.0% and the five-year-plus rate at 3.5%, both unchanged from the prior month, leaving LPR flat for an 18th consecutive month. The third-quarter monetary policy report proposed pushing reform of the loan pricing benchmark, moving away from pricing solely off LPR toward a more diversified set of benchmarks, and guiding financial institutions to explore floating-rate loans linked to DR001.

**Commentary**: LPR has been on hold for 18 months and the market is waiting for the rate cut to land. But the more interesting signal is the direction toward DR001-linked pricing. Shifting the benchmark from LPR to a shorter rate that tracks money market conditions more closely means floating-rate loans will follow real funding costs more tightly, which actually makes borrowing costs more predictable. If you carry floating-rate debt, this is a good moment to renegotiate your pricing terms.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Global Events

**US-China**: On October 25 the US and Chinese trade teams held a new round of negotiations in Kuala Lumpur, focused on Section 301 measures in shipbuilding, the suspension period for tariffs, fentanyl enforcement cooperation, agricultural purchases, export controls and rare earths. On November 1 the White House issued a statement announcing a broad economic agreement reached between President Trump, in Korea, and President Xi Jinping, covering tariff adjustments, critical minerals trade and agricultural purchases. The US side then extended the tariff exemption period on technology transfers to China, and the two sides have kept senior-level contact on trade issues.

**Commentary**: The gap between a framework agreement and actual implementation is where the real work sits. What matters is not the wording of the text, but how long the tariff exemptions last and whether the export control list for critical minerals expands. For anyone in cross-border trade, the practical move is to re-audit the origin rules: transshipment paths, third-country processing qualifications and how origin is determined. If any one of those three is wrong, the tariff cost on the whole shipment goes from near zero to near zero in reverse.

**China-EU trade**: From October 22 the EU repealed the definitive anti-dumping and countervailing duties on solar glass originating in China, covering a combined 84.2% rate, and qualifying importers can apply for refunds of duties paid during the review period without interest. But the tightening is happening at the same time: from November 12 an EU implementing regulation imposed provisional anti-dumping duties on copper tubes from China, Mexico, Vietnam and Uzbekistan, with rates on Chinese goods reaching 51.5%.

**Commentary**: In the same window, one 84.2% duty disappears and a new 51.5% one appears. EU trade defense toward China is not wholesale easing or wholesale decoupling, it is precise adjustment product by product. So the right strategy for Chinese exporters is not to guess the overall wind direction, it is to compute the rate on every product line: which categories Europe simply cannot source without Chinese supply (rates fall), and which ones Europe or third countries can substitute (rates rise). Copper tubes and solar glass are a clean pair of samples, one in each direction.

**Geopolitics**: The situation around the Strait of Hormuz has evolved from straightforward military harassment into a compound crisis in which institutional blockade and military deterrence coexist. The commander of the Iranian Army stated explicitly that the strait will not be opened until seven conditions are met, and that the blockade will tighten further, with strait access tied directly to geopolitical demands.

**Commentary**: This is the item worth taking most seriously in this briefing. Passage through the strait has shifted from being driven by safety risk to being politically priced, which means shipping costs, insurance premiums and arrival times all need to be recomputed under new rules rather than historical averages. Energy prices are already an explicit reason for the ECB's rate hikes. Any company with shipping, cross-border trade or energy procurement should now write a Strait disruption scenario into the formal risk register, instead of treating it as a news headline that passed.
