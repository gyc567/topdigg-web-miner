The biggest variable this morning is not in the tape, it is in who sets the price. On the same Friday that Nvidia broke down 2.01% with RSI collapsing to 19.1, Treasury Secretary Bessent publicly demanded a "dramatic shakeup" at the company and urged it to hire an outside CEO, while Anthropic's IPO landed in the same week. Hardware conviction is ceding the floor to application pricing. If you are still paying a premium for compute hardware, the next three months will be uncomfortable.

## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

### 🦅 Federal Reserve

On 28 October the FOMC kept the federal funds target range at 3.75%–4.00% on a 10–2 vote, with Cook and Hammack dissenting in favour of a 25 basis point hike. The statement said inflation remains elevated, job gains have kept pace with the workforce and productivity growth is strong. October CPI rose 0.4% month on month, above expectations, October producer prices rose 0.3% month on month and 6.5% year on year, the highest in nearly four years, and October payrolls added 165,000 with unemployment slipping to 4.1%.

**Takeaway**: This is the most hawkish version of doing nothing. Two voters openly wanted to hike, so the road to rate cuts is effectively closed and the direction over the coming months is hold or tighten, not ease. If you carry dollar debt, locking rates today is cheaper than locking them three months from now. The Fed's own latest survey naming AI risk as the single biggest threat to the financial system explains its reluctance to bail out better than any verbal reassurance.

### 🏰 European Central Bank

On 29 October the Governing Council left all three key rates unchanged, deposit facility at 2.50%, main refinancing operations at 2.65% and marginal lending at 2.90%. The statement explicitly flags rising energy prices as an upside risk to inflation and a downside risk to growth, and notes that financing conditions have tightened since the previous meeting. The ECB wage tracker points to slightly higher wage growth in the first three quarters of 2027 than in full-year 2026.

**Takeaway**: The ECB is in a worse spot than the Fed. The Fed faces demand that is too hot, the ECB faces supply-side inflation from an energy shock, which locks its rate-cut space. With a 2.50% deposit rate, policy is already near the floor. Energy prices have become Europe's most important macro variable, and European chemicals, autos, logistics and airlines will all have a slice of their profit statements eaten by energy costs over the next two quarters.

### 🇨🇳 People's Bank of China

October domestic producer prices rose at their fastest pace in more than four years while consumer prices kept weakening, the classic pattern of upstream inflation and downstream softness. Policy has stayed deliberately silent on the currency, with both onshore and offshore yuan holding near 6.67.

**Takeaway**: Producer prices up and consumer prices down means the central bank has no need to cut or to tighten, because the inflation pressure sits upstream rather than at the consumer, so monetary room is wider than markets assume. For A-shares that means upstream cyclicals hold up better while high-multiple growth gets squeezed on both cost and valuation. If you want to position in resources, the window is now, not in three months.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Geopolitics

**US-China: the chip war escalates ahead of the leaders' meeting.** Bessent said publicly that the two countries cooperate and compete in AI and that neither side should "harbor no illusions", while accusing the Commerce Department of chip "leakage" to China. The Jensen Huang spy allegation has revived calls to tighten export controls.

**Takeaway**: Chip export controls have stopped being a technical issue and become a negotiating chip, and the closer the leaders' meeting gets, the more likely the escalation comes first and the negotiation second. Hardware supply chain operators should leave room for another tightening round over the next two quarters and stop treating a single customer path as a moat.

**China-Europe trade**: China's goods surplus topped 1 trillion dollars for a second consecutive year, with sustained US and European pressure on Beijing to rebalance trade. The ECB explicitly lists the energy shock as a downside risk to growth.

**Takeaway**: A trillion-dollar surplus is a Chinese competitiveness problem and also a geopolitical fuse. Countermeasures, anti-dumping probes and additional tariffs will only get more frequent. For sellers going into Europe, the job for the next year is not opening more stores, it is reshaping the market structure from a single EU pole into an EU plus Middle East plus Latin America portfolio, because revenue should not sit entirely in one market that is actively pressuring China.

**Geopolitics: the energy and shipping risk premium is repricing.** Iran struck the US base in Bahrain, anti-Houthi forces retreated in Yemen, and disruption to shipping in the Strait of Hormuz is directly lifting global fertilizer costs and food security concerns. Japan's economy unexpectedly contracted in the third quarter with high energy costs as the main cause.

**Takeaway**: What makes this round of conflict different from 2024 is that it lands directly on energy and fertilizer prices. Fertilizer up means grain up, grain up means global inflation becomes sticky again, and that means the Fed cannot cut. Geopolitics ultimately transmits to your cost of capital through the rate cut path. Japan's contraction with the yen stuck around 159.75 is a clear warning: energy dependent economies have no answer when energy shocks hit.
