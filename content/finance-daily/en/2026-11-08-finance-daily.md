## 🏛 Central Banks & Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve**: On Oct 28 the FOMC kept the federal funds target range at 3.75%-4.00% on a 10-2 vote, with Cook and Hammack dissenting in favor of a rate hike. The statement says inflation remains elevated and adds that the Committee will deliver price stability.
Takeaway: This is a hawkish dissent that has been rare in recent years. Nobody is arguing for a cut; two voters think the Fed has cut too much already. Combined with September PCE at 3.6% YoY and Q3 GDP at 3.4%, the economy is neither weak nor disinflationary. December is very likely a hold, and any lingering expectation of further easing should be priced out entirely.

🏰 **ECB**: Eurozone flash inflation rose to 4.0% in October from 3.8% in September, the highest since September 2023, with energy prices up 20.8% YoY. Lagarde gave an interview to Yomiuri Shimbun on Nov 7, and Schnabel steps down from the Executive Board in early January.
Takeaway: Eurozone inflation at 4% with energy as the main driver leaves the ECB with essentially no room to cut. The euro slipping below 1.12 isn't about European strength. It's the market discounting high inflation and a central bank that won't respond. Stay out of euro assets in the near term.

🇨🇳 **PBOC**: The Oct 20 LPR fixing held at 3.00% for the 1-year tenor and 3.50% for the 5-year-plus tenor, the 12th consecutive month of no change. Offshore yuan has been trading in a 6.67-6.70 band.
Takeaway: Twelve straight months of holding is unusual in Chinese monetary policy history. The message is clear: policy is waiting, waiting for the Fed's path to clarify and external constraints to loosen. That's good news for business owners and bad news for people trading narratives. Without incremental rate-cut expectations, you won't get a broad-based re-rating. Structural tools will keep targeting specific sectors, and only specific sectors.

## 🌐 Diplomacy & Major Events

**US-China**: Democrats swept both chambers in the midterms, setting up a White House-Congress policy fight that starts in January. The direct market consequence is that the $41.1T federal debt ceiling becomes the main storyline of 2027, with the federal deficit already ballooning to $2T, up 12% YoY. Meanwhile, roughly 600,000 NVIDIA H200 chips are stranded in Hong Kong because Beijing has delayed large-scale import approval, allowing only partial batches to the largest internet groups (about 10,000 each). Separately, the US, Russia and China are discussing what would be the first-ever Trump-Putin-Xi trilateral summit, proposed by Putin in an Oct 9 call, aimed at finding a way to end the war in Ukraine.
Takeaway: Chips stuck in a port, a summit on the table. That is the complete picture of US-China relations right now: technically squeezing each other, politically looking for an off-ramp. If you build AI products, watch the H200 stockpile closely, because once those clear there will be a price war between domestic Chinese compute and Nvidia. If you do cross-border trade, the window on tariffs and licensing sits in the next six months.

**China-Europe**: Amazon announced more than €16B of investment in Germany for 2027, including €4.8B for the AWS Frankfurt region, spanning cloud, data centers, satellite internet and logistics. At the same time, eurozone inflation reached 4.0%, the highest since September 2023.
Takeaway: That €16B is a political check for Europe. In an election year with inflation spiking, capital buys stability, and stability buys the government's promise not to tear up data center regulation. Chinese companies going overseas should take notes on the playbook of trading capital for regulatory certainty.

**Geopolitics**: Iran and the Strait of Hormuz remain tied to energy pricing, visible directly in the 20.8% YoY rise in eurozone energy prices. A second signal deserves attention: voters in at least 14 Ohio towns passed bans or new limits on data centers in the midterms, with 10 towns outright banning large facilities. Across the US, 41 data center measures appeared on ballots, and Ohio accounted for 21 of them.
Takeaway: This is the first time AI compute expansion has been hard-blocked by voters. The compute narrative ran for two years and the market treated it as politically unassailable. Ohio just said otherwise with its ballots. If this spreads, power, water and grid capacity become the real ceiling on compute expansion, not GPU supply. Anyone treating AI infrastructure capex as a risk-free growth story needs to re-run the compliance math on siting.
