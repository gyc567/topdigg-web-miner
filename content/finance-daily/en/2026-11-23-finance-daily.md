Today's market produced a rare combination: **gold and silver at record highs while US tech stocks collapsed.** This is not a "rate cut rescue" story. This is an "inflation plus rate hikes" story.

For founders and operators, that leads to one very concrete conclusion: **stop counting on easy liquidity to rescue your valuation. Cash and real orders are worth more than narratives.**

## 📈 US Equities Focus

(Closing prices from Nov 20, markets closed over the weekend)

**🍎 Apple (AAPL)**: $342.56 (-1.90%), market cap $4.98 trillion, P/E 38.15, price target $345.63, RSI 46.45.

Apple's decline was mild, essentially a beta move with the broader tape. UBS noted that iPhone Duo wait times shortened to about 53 days week over week, and that number deserves attention: shorter wait times mean weaker demand and loosening channel inventory. The 45-analyst consensus rating is still "Buy", but the $345.63 target is essentially the current price, which means the sell side has no room left to raise. When analysts see 0.9% upside on a five-trillion-dollar company, the odds simply do not work.

**🚗 Tesla (TSLA)**: $345.82 (-7.64%), market cap $1.37 trillion, P/E 379.95, price target $417.53, RSI 37.78.

Of the three, Tesla's drop has the cleanest logic. The market is questioning the actual benefit of the reported merger with SpaceX, and HSBC upgraded to Hold while setting a target of only $359. The valuation is the bigger problem: a 379x trailing P/E and 13x EV/EBITDA leaves no cushion in a rising cost-of-capital environment. A company holding a $1.37 trillion valuation on a 2030 autonomous-driving narrative has no defensive ground when rate-hike expectations return.

**🔥 NVIDIA (NVDA)**: $125.53 (-14.01%), market cap $3.03 trillion, P/E 13.48, price target $244.18, RSI 15.05.

This is today's most striking number. An RSI of 15.05 is textbook oversold, and the 63-analyst target of $244.18 is nearly double the current price. But note: oversold is not a buy signal. A low RSI only tells you selling pressure has been thoroughly released.

The real reasons behind NVDA's plunge are three overlapping items: US securities regulators are investigating the company's accounting practices; Jensen Huang said he would be "honored" to return as CEO, but the board refused to reinstate him, exposing a governance fracture; and Representative McCaul is publicly pressing Huang for answers on chip sales and whether advanced AI technology reached China.

Q3 results themselves were a blockbuster, with a beat that was already priced into consensus. So the issue was never earnings. It is governance, it is regulators, it is geopolitics. You cannot console yourself with "the numbers are great so it will come back." If you hold AI-theme exposure, this is the moment to trim, not to add.

## 🥇 🥈 Commodities

**Gold**: $3572.4/oz (+0.91%), intraday range $3569.1 - $3585.5, extending record highs.
**Silver**: $42.09/oz (+1.99%), intraday range $42.03 - $42.34, outperforming gold.

**Takeaway**: Silver's gain ran at nearly twice gold's while gold itself never gave back ground. That is not haven buying. That is inflation-hedging buying. The macro logic is straightforward: the Fed's October minutes show nearly all officials thinking more hikes are needed, while the ECB warns that persistently high energy prices could push inflation higher. Under that combination, people buying gold and silver are not buying "safety" — they are buying an inflation hedge.

**Crude oil**: slipped below $83 per barrel, a fourth straight weekly decline. The IEA forecast a global supply surplus of about 2 million barrels per day this quarter, and Goldman Sachs warned an even larger oversupply could emerge next year.

**Takeaway**: Here is the counterintuitive and genuinely important point: the Middle East is at war and oil is still falling. Tankers in the Strait of Hormuz have been attacked, Iran struck Bahrain over the weekend, and US assets in neighbouring countries remain targets, yet crude keeps sliding because the supply surplus story outweighs the geopolitical premium.

For operators, that is a direct conclusion: if your cost base tracks oil (chemicals, logistics, plastics, fibres), the next one to two quarters are a cost-improvement window. Do not write an energy-cost increase into your quotes in advance. And if your costs are energy-insensitive, do not expect conflict to raise your margins for you.

## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

**🦅 The Fed**: federal funds rate at 3.75%. The October FOMC minutes (released Nov 18) show that nearly all participants judged further action to raise the federal funds rate would likely be appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to 2%, with a couple of participants noting that an increase of more than 25bps could warrant consideration if incoming inflation data were unfavourable. Next FOMC meeting: Dec 8-9.

**Takeaway**: This is the first time since 2022 that the language has pointed collectively toward hikes. The market's December pricing has shifted from wait-and-see to a binary choice between hiking and not hiking, and the scale is tipping toward hiking. If you are planning 2027 financing, the December meeting is a node you must watch, not something to adjust to after the fact.

**🏰 The ECB**: at its October meeting the ECB held rates, keeping the main refinancing rate at 2.65% and the deposit facility rate at 2.50%. The bank warned that stubbornly high energy prices could push up inflation while dragging on economic activity, particularly through indirect and second-round effects, although underlying inflation stayed broadly stable with no significant wage response so far. President Lagarde announced plans to step down early, drawing attention amid volatility in the French bond market. After the meeting Lagarde confirmed that the ECB had discussed a rate hike before agreeing unanimously to hold.

**Takeaway**: The four words "discussed a hike" matter far more than "held rates." Combined with Lagarde's early departure, the ECB sits in the most fragile configuration for eurozone assets: a possible policy pivot stacked on a leadership transition. If you do European market entry, European compliance, or euro settlement, uncertainty rises visibly over the next two quarters.

**🇨🇳 The PBoC**: LPR was left unchanged for the 18th consecutive month in November, with the one-year rate at 3.0% and the five-year rate at 3.5%, in line with expectations. October industrial output and retail sales growth moderated and home prices kept falling, but stronger-than-expected Q3 growth and a firmer yuan reduced the urgency for further easing. The Q3 monetary policy report reiterated an "appropriately loose" stance with flexibility.

**Takeaway**: The PBoC is waiting. Waiting for the Fed to move first, waiting for confirmation from the Q3 growth data. Rising US rate expectations plus a PBoC on hold means the China-US rate differential keeps narrowing, which is exactly why the offshore yuan firmed slightly. For domestic companies: funding costs will not get cheaper soon, but they will not get more expensive either. The real variable is December. If you carry floating-rate loans, put December on your repayment calendar now.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Major Events

**China-US leaders' diplomacy and trade**: during Xi Jinping's state visit to the United States from Sep 23-25, 2026, the two sides reached an eight-point consensus, including building a "constructive and strategic stabilizing relationship based on respect, fairness and reciprocity," establishing and advancing mechanisms such as the Trade Council, agreeing a "$30 billion for $30 billion" reciprocal tariff reduction arrangement, and deferring the outcomes of the Kuala Lumpur economic and trade consultation. On Nov 13, China and the United States held the first video meeting of the Trade Council under the China-US economic and trade consultation mechanism, guided by the important consensus of the leaders' meeting, and conducted candid, in-depth and constructive exchanges on tariffs, the "30 billion for 30 billion" reciprocal tariff reduction arrangement, agricultural trade, and other topics of mutual interest.

**Takeaway**: This is the most certain line on the board right now. The "$30 billion for $30 billion" reciprocal tariff cut is moving from a leaders' consensus into the execution list of the Trade Council. For export factories, cross-border e-commerce teams, and agricultural and commodity traders, this is real money. But watch the pacing: the fine print has not landed, so do not book the tariff cut into your Q1 revenue model. The move worth making today is to map which HS codes in your own product catalogue fall under the cut, so that when the details arrive you are the first to react rather than the last.

**China-EU trade**: EU tariffs and anti-subsidy measures on Chinese electric vehicles and related sectors remain the most concentrated friction point in the China-EU relationship, with negotiations over alternatives such as minimum price commitments still deadlocked.

**Takeaway**: Here I will be straightforward — real-time search was restricted for this edition, so the above is compiled from recent public background and you should defer to official announcements. The direction is not in doubt: the electric vehicle dispute will not be settled quickly, and any supply chain dependent on the single EU market needs a non-European alternative. That is no longer a contingency plan. It belongs on the cost sheet.

**Geopolitics**: Middle East tensions remain elevated, with more tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and Iranian strikes on Bahrain over the weekend alongside continued targeting of US assets in neighbouring countries. Yet crude slipped below $83 per barrel for a fourth straight week, because the IEA forecast a global supply surplus of around 2 million barrels per day this quarter and Goldman Sachs warned of a larger oversupply next year.

**Takeaway**: This is today's most counterintuitive and most valuable read. The harder the Middle East fights, the lower oil goes, because the supply-side story is stronger. For operators the practical implication is that energy and shipping costs are most likely to drift lower over the next one to two quarters, even though real transit risk in Hormuz will push insurance costs up. The transmission chain "conflict leads to inflation leads to central bank hikes" has had one of its links broken by oversupply in 2026. Stop mechanically running the old playbook.
