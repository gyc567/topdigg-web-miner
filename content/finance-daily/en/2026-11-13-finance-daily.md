## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve**: NY Fed President Williams spoke repeatedly Beijing time this morning, saying inflation is "stubborn and elevated," that the Fed needs to act over time to bring inflation back to 2%, that policy still has "work to do," and that action must not damage the labor market. The 2-year Treasury yield rose to a one-month high of 4.892%. The Fed also published adjusted tentative 2027 FOMC meeting schedules on November 12.

Take: This is the classic "I want to go further but I'm afraid to break employment" line. This is the most uncomfortable state the market can be in: too afraid to cut, too afraid to price a hike. Until the discount rate on growth assets comes down, multiple expansion cannot start. For anyone holding AI hardware, compute, or long-duration assets, cash cost over the next two quarters matters far more than the valuation story.

🏰 **ECB**: No new rate decision this week. The most recent public remarks came from Philip Lane (monetary policy communication) and Piero Cipollone (cash and the digital euro), with no clear policy shift signal. EUR/USD at 1.10527, down 0.37% on the day, with the euro squeezed from both sides by a hawkish Fed and Europe's own growth fatigue.

Take: Europe's problem has never been policy, it is growth. While the euro is sliding, there will be no re-rating of European assets. Do not rush to buy Europe cheap.

🇨🇳 **PBOC**: No new policy moves recently. November 20 is the next LPR fixing date. Onshore yuan at 6.6745, offshore at 6.6718, essentially flat intraday, with the currency giving the market no directional signal.

Take: Stabilizing the currency is taking priority over supporting growth, and countercyclical tools are being held back. That is unhelpful for names that live on liquidity and narrative, and irrelevant for hard tech with real orders. For operating businesses this combination is actually good news, because it means policy is leaving room for structural transition.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Major Events

**US-China leaders and trade**: Nvidia founder and CEO Jensen Huang formally announced his resignation on November 12. The board appointed an interim CEO and launched a search for a permanent successor. SCMP reports the candidate pool includes internal executives, competitors, and "Trump's people." Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott has written to Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick demanding a hearing next month. The New York Times reports Trump privately urged Huang to "hold on to his position" before the resignation. SCMP's read: this may signal tighter US restrictions on advanced AI chip exports to China.

Take: Connect the two stories and the logic snaps into place. Nvidia is not just a company, it is the policy interface through which Washington controls advanced compute flows to China, and when the person at the interface changes, the rules at the interface get rewritten. For anyone running Chinese AI compute, chip, or server businesses, this is not sentiment, it is order structure: the window for domestic substitution has opened while the uncertainty around imported compute has widened. Do not frame this as good or bad, this is a repricing of the supply chain.

**China-EU trade**: No new large-scale EU anti-dumping case against China landed in this window. Europe's real change is that the pricing of "de-risking" and industrial security is accelerating. After US tech controls spilled over, European automakers and Tier 1 suppliers face rising costs across data, compute, and compliance. Meanwhile global infrastructure investment has been weaponized, with the US providing $450,000 in military aid to push the expansion of Callao port in Peru while the rivalry with China over Chancay port continues. Ports, rail, and grids are reverting to geopolitical bargaining chips.

Take: Europe is not your growth market right now, at least not that. Your growth is in Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Latin America, and the share of domestic demand being displaced by import substitution. To judge whether a market is worth entering, look at three things: demand growth, barriers to entry, and whether you hold an irreplaceable position. Europe scores none of the three.

**Geopolitics**: The Iran war continues. The Houthis claim strikes on Saudi energy and military facilities, Tehran warns it will widen the retaliation range, and fighting in Yemen is escalating. WHO says more than 320,000 people have been displaced since August 1. Trump says the US does not need a deal with Iran. On Russia and Ukraine, following trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi, US representatives met separately with Putin and Zelensky, and the next round is reportedly broader and will include European partners.

Take: The geopolitical premium in oil and the safe-haven premium in gold are both still live. Do not treat either as "risk already priced in." The risk pricing is concentrated in crude: Brent at $96.

**Also worth watching**: Microsoft's Microsoft 365, Outlook, and Teams applications were down for several hours on November 12 US Eastern time, also affecting part of OpenAI's infrastructure, with Microsoft limiting traffic types in some affected regions. TSMC announced that data on some current and former employees appeared on external websites, with no intrusion into its own systems, pointing to a cloud service provider.
