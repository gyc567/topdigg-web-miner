## 🏛 Central Banks & Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve:**
The Fed hiked rates by 25bps in September, raising the federal funds target range to 3.75%-4.00% — its first rate increase since 2023. The FOMC voted 12-0, citing continued solid economic growth, resilient domestic spending, and strong productivity gains. The committee aims for a timely return to the 2% inflation target. Next meeting in December.

🏰 **European Central Bank:**
The ECB also hiked 25bps in September, raising all three rates: deposit facility to 2.50%, main refinancing to 2.65%, and marginal lending facility to 2.90% (effective September 16). The ECB emphasized "data-dependent, meeting-by-meeting" decisions with no preset path. Next rate decision: October 29. Growth forecasts revised up to 0.9% for 2026.

🇨🇳 **People's Bank of China:**
September LPR held steady for the 16th consecutive month: 1-year at 3.00%, 5-year+ at 3.50%. Exports growing 20%+ YoY for three straight months supports the economy; August CPI and PPI rebounded, manufacturing PMI returned above 50. Analysts expect rate cuts/reserve requirement cuts still possible before year-end, but short-term priority is maintaining liquidity for government bond issuance.

## 🌐 Diplomacy & Major Events

**Global Central Bank Synchronized Tightening:**
The Fed and ECB both raised rates in September while China held LPR steady, reflecting converging views on economic resilience. Policy divergence among major central banks has narrowed. The US dollar remains elevated, and emerging market capital outflow pressures warrant close monitoring.

**Geopolitical Risks Continue to Weigh:**
Middle East tensions and the Russia-Ukraine conflict continue to disrupt global supply chains and energy prices. Major central banks are walking a tightrope between combating inflation and maintaining growth, making geopolitical uncertainty the core variable in global asset allocation for the second half of the year.
