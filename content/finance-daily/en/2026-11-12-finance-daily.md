## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve**: base rate 3.75%–4.00%. The 10/28 FOMC left it unchanged, but two voters argued on the spot for a 25 basis point hike. the CPI for month 10 published on 11/10 came in above expectations, headline month-on-month +0.4% and core month-on-month +0.3%, and the market pushed the probability of a hike at the 12/8-9 FOMC to close to 100%. Bank of America warned that a 50 basis point move in one go is possible, Deutsche Bank expects three hikes in a row, and Goldman Sachs changed its tune to say the hiking cycle is not over yet and there could be another 25 basis points in the 3rd month of next year.

Take: This is not the end of a cutting cycle, this is the beginning of a hiking cycle. For an operator this means two things. The global cost of dollar funding steps up, and the dollar debt and hedging costs of outbound businesses both get more expensive. At the same time the "asset scarcity" logic reverses, and asset valuations that were pushed up by liquidity have to be recalculated. Do not take a big leveraged bet before 12/8.

🏰 **ECB**: deposit facility rate 2.50% (effective 2026/9/16), main refinancing operations rate 2.65%, and the most recent monetary policy press conference was on 10/29.

Take: The ECB is still in its cutting channel while the Fed turns to hiking, the US and Europe rate gap widens passively, and pressure on the euro is the natural result. 1.11 is the pressure level, so for cross-border procurement and for European market expansion, the conversion window on the renminbi and euro cost side needs close attention.

🇨🇳 **PBOC**: the 2026 monetary policy stance is "moderately loose", and the government work report explicitly states "lower policy rates and the reserve requirement ratio". The LPR has been on hold for several months in a row, and expectations for a reserve requirement cut and rate cuts within the year are still heating up.

Take: The policy-cycle mismatch between China and the US is both the support for the yuan and the constraint on it. The rate tools have not moved for now, which means the priority is currency stability over growth stability. That runs against what the market broadly expects, which is an immediate rate cut, so do not mistake policy expectations for policy landing.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Major Events

**China-US leaders and diplomacy**: On 11/6, the Chinese lead negotiator He Lifeng held a video call with the US lead negotiator, US Treasury Secretary Bessent and Trade Representative Greer. The two sides exchanged frank and in-depth views on implementing the understandings reached by the two heads of state and on the results of the economic and trade consultations, with the US side stressing that China should deliver on its earlier agricultural purchase commitments. At the same time, the US is shifting the focus of its tariff pressure toward the EU, Japan, Vietnam and the Philippines. Assessment: China and the US have entered a phase where the heads of state set the tone and ministers execute, friction is shifting from all-out confrontation to point-by-point price pressure, and China's purchase commitments on the two lines of agricultural products and manufacturing exports are the window worth watching next.

**China-EU trade**: The joint statement on the China-EU economic and trade consultations on 11/9 listed 16 areas of agreed outcomes, with both sides confirming that they will advance trade in hybrid vehicles and cooperation on export controls within a framework consistent with WTO rules. Data for month 10 shows the container fill rate on China-Europe rail services holding at 100% for 50 consecutive months, with a marked drop in overall logistics costs. The former President of the European Commission publicly said not to expect the US to return to free trade and called for stronger China-EU economic ties. Assessment: this is the most pragmatic structural cooperation under the tariff wall, and it is more actionable than the EU's posture on electric vehicles. If you are in cross-border logistics, hybrid vehicle components, or product categories sensitive to export controls, these 16 consensus points are worth checking line by line against your own product codes.

**Geopolitics**: The Strait of Hormuz keeps having incidents. On 11/8 a tanker that violated the rules struck a mine and caught fire, on 11/10 two tankers exploded in the strait, and on 11/11 tanker attacks reappeared, with crude oil prices rebounding in response. Iran says the "two-day plan" is still in force, and passage for non-hostile ships requires coordination with the Iranian side. LNG transit through the strait in month 10 hit a record high since the Iran war broke out. Assessment: this waterway carries roughly 20% of global oil and LNG shipments, so the question is no longer "will it be closed" but "how high will the cost of passage go". If you are in energy, chemicals, shipping or marine insurance, this cost line has to be repriced.
