## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

🦅 **Federal Reserve**: The Oct 28 FOMC kept the federal funds target range at 3.75%-4.00% in a 10-2 vote, extending the 25bp hike delivered in September, the first increase since 2023. The statement said economic activity is expanding strongly and employment is keeping pace with the labor force, but inflation remains elevated and geopolitical developments leave uncertainty unusually high. Chair Warsh explicitly rejected "forward guidance" after the meeting and announced the Fed will reduce the number of policy meetings with voting members starting in 2027.

**Takeaway**: This is a central bank that has moved from data dependence to data bondage. Without a stated path, the market guesses the next move at every meeting, and volatility stays permanently overpriced. For corporate treasurers and cross-border trade, hedging the rate is more useful than forecasting it.

🏰 **European Central Bank**: The October meeting kept the main refinancing rate at 2.65% and the deposit facility rate at 2.50%, but Lagarde confirmed afterward that a rate hike was actually debated and a consensus to hold was reached. The ECB warned that persistently high energy prices could push inflation higher through indirect and second-round effects while dragging on activity. Reports also say Lagarde announced she will step down early, right as French bond market volatility intensifies.

**Takeaway**: This is the most fragile corner of the global monetary market. A hike is sitting on the table and the leadership is changing hands, so the "policy uncertainty premium" on eurozone assets persists. The euro's recent flatness is not stability, it is the absence of direction.

🇨🇳 **People's Bank of China**: October LPR stayed unchanged for the 17th straight month, with the one-year rate at 3.0% and the five-year rate at 3.5%, both at historic lows. Q3 GDP grew 4.5% year-on-year, ahead of expectations, exports stayed strong, and both CPI and PPI turned up in August, all of which reduces the urgency for further easing. Pan Gongsheng said the central bank would advance reforms to interest rate pricing benchmarks.

**Takeaway**: Holding rates is not the same as doing nothing. A firm currency and stable growth have temporarily locked up the room for monetary easing. For operators, financing costs will not fall faster near term; the real thing to watch is where fiscal stimulus lands.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Major Events

**US-China**: The two heads of state met in Beijing in May 2026 and agreed to establish a government-to-government trade council. The eighth round of economic and trade talks was held in New York and Washington on Sept 20-23, producing consensus on the council's operating rules, the division of responsibilities for the "30 billion for 30 billion" reciprocal tariff-reduction framework, and the list of products covered. Both sides agreed to cut tariffs on roughly $30 billion of imports from each other on a reciprocal basis, with about 90% of those products moving to Most Favored Nation rates.

**Takeaway**: The negotiation machinery now works, which is a genuine change from the tariff-war phase. But $30 billion on each side is a rounding error in the bilateral trade book. The real test is whether the next round expands the list or stalls. Supply-chain operators: do not sign long-term orders until you check whether this list actually covers you.

**EU-China**: EU trade defense is accelerating. On Oct 30 the Commission imposed anti-dumping duties on imports of PET resin spunbond products, and on Nov 6 it initiated a safeguard investigation into PET resin chemicals to assess whether safeguard measures are needed to protect EU industry from an import surge. On Nov 5 the Commission led a delegation of European medical device companies to Shanghai to pursue new business in the Chinese market.

**Takeaway**: Levying duties on one front while leading a sales delegation into China on another is the EU's actual position right now. It is tightening defensively while continuing to push into the growth market. For Chinese exporters, intermediate goods such as PET and textiles are the exposed segment, and the line to watch is safeguards, which are worse than anti-dumping because they can be manufactured out of thin air.

**Geopolitics**: The US-Iran conflict has entered its eighth month and is escalating again. On Nov 8 Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi warned that Tehran will "rain down hell" if the United States resumes attacks, and reiterated that his two-day proposal, demanding a halt to the war on all fronts, still stands. President Trump said US forces are "locked and loaded" and ready to strike. Qeshm Island and the Strait of Hormuz have both been hit, with the US blamed for strikes on Qeshm, and drones have struck northern Iraq, while mediators continue to work the file.

**Takeaway**: This is the week's biggest risk source. A real closure of Hormuz reprices crude, marine insurance, fertilizer and chemicals all at once, inflation expectations rise again, and the Fed and ECB have even less room to cut. **What operators should do now**: if you have Iranian customers or suppliers, review delivery terms and force majeure clauses this week. Do not wait for the incident to start the conversation.
