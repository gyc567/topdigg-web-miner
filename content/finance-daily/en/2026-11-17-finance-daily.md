The whole story today fits in one line: **the market replaced the assumption of rate cuts with rate hikes**. October producer prices rose 6.5 percent year on year, consumer prices rose 0.4 percent on the month, and payrolls came in at 165,000 with unemployment falling to 4.1 percent. Stack those three numbers and the dollar hits a 19-month high while every risk asset gets repriced.

If you invoice in dollars, convert more deliberately. If you buy from overseas suppliers, your negotiating position just got stronger.

**Read:** Gold and silver fell together, with silver down almost 4 percent, and that combination is unusual. Normally when fear rises gold goes first and silver follows it up. Right now the dollar and real yields are rising at the same time, so precious metals are being squeezed out by liquidity, not by a change in the fear trade. Silver falling nearly four times as much as gold says **speculative positioning is being liquidated, not allocation money buying**. Gold is still the ballast for a diversified balance sheet, but this is not the moment to add. When the dollar is stronger, gold goes down with it.

## 🏛 Central Banks and Policy

**🦅 The Federal Reserve**
October producer prices rose 0.3 percent on the month and 6.5 percent year on year, the highest in nearly four years. October consumer prices rose 0.4 percent, above expectations. October payrolls added 165,000 jobs and unemployment slipped to 4.1 percent. Sticky inflation plus a resilient labour market pushed rate cut expectations straight over to the hike side, which is why the dollar hit a 19-month high. Separately, a Fed survey names **artificial intelligence risk as the largest threat to the financial system**, which is worth writing down: supervisors now treat AI as the next candidate source of systemic risk.

**🏰 The European Central Bank**
No new rate decision this week, but officials were busy. Elderson spoke on smart supervision for small banks, Vujčić on risks and resilience in the euro area financial system, and Schnabel had recently framed monetary policy as navigating stormy waters. The ECB wage tracker suggests slightly higher wage growth in the first three quarters of 2027 than in full-year 2026. There is no sign of inflation running away in the euro area, but growth cannot keep up, and with the Fed turning hawkish the euro has nowhere to go but down.

**🇨🇳 The People's Bank of China**
China's October producer prices rose at the fastest pace in more than four years while consumer inflation slowed. The goods trade surplus exceeded 1 trillion dollars for a second straight year, and calls for Beijing to rebalance the trade structure continue. The yuan is holding near 6.66, and policy is clearly prioritizing stability. **A trillion-dollar surplus, a stable currency, and rising producer prices** are a tailwind for exporters, while companies that depend on imported raw materials should watch for cost pressure.

## 🌐 Diplomacy and Global Events

**US-China summit diplomacy**
China has confirmed an upcoming historic three-way summit between Xi, Trump and Putin, with trade and security on the agenda. US Treasury Secretary Bessent said publicly that the two countries cooperate and compete in artificial intelligence at the same time and that neither side should harbor illusions. Separately, media reported that after Jensen Huang gained access to the White House, Trump family accounts saw gains of up to 97.5 million dollars on Nvidia. The number is not the point for an operator. The point is that **chip export policy has become a bargaining chip in a multi-party contest rather than a plain industrial policy**.

**China-Europe trade**
China's goods surplus topped 1 trillion dollars for a second year, which is the ammunition Europe and the United States are using to press for a rebalancing. The US Federal Communications Commission plans to bar Chinese testing laboratories, and analysts warn this could disrupt global electronics supply chains and raise industry costs. For anyone running cross-border commerce or hardware: **compliance cost is migrating from tariffs to testing and certification**, and many companies have not put that in the budget yet.

**Geopolitics**
Middle East exports are recovering and crude has fallen back to about 85 dollars a barrel. More than 3,000 protests have taken place in the United States against military operations against Iran. Estonia, a NATO member, will close its border with Russia after an attempted sabotage targeting a drone maker. The IMF chief warns that the global economy is standing on one leg, with circular financing around artificial intelligence now at 1 trillion dollars.
