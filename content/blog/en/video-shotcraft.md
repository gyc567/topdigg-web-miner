# Video Shotcraft: The AI Product Video Revolution

## Introduction

In the digital marketing era, product videos have become one of the most direct and effective ways for brands to reach users. However, traditional video production workflows often take weeks, requiring professional teams, expensive equipment, and complex project management.

**Video Shotcraft** is redefining product video production. It's an AI agent skill that transforms Claude Code or Codex into a motion-design studio—describe your product and it automatically storyboards, animates, and sound-designs cinematic promo videos.

## I. Core Design Philosophy: Turn AI into a Motion Design Studio

### 1.1 Core Principle

The most important design philosophy of Video Shotcraft is **turning AI into a professional motion design tool**, not a simple video generator.

What does this mean? Shotcraft doesn't generate random video clips; it provides:
- **157 meticulously designed shot recipe cards**
- **214 motion style previews**
- Built on professional motion design principles
- Supports real page captures and 2.5D camera moves

### 1.2 Professional Motion Design Language

Shotcraft's shot recipes were distilled from the motion language of official product videos from ClickUp, Perplexity, Slack, Notion, Figma, Framer, Bear, Raycast, Pitch, Miro, Superhuman, and Loom.

### 1.3 Declarative Production Workflow

Shotcraft uses a **declarative** production approach:
- Users declare goals and constraints
- AI handles execution details
- Each stage has clear acceptance criteria

## II. 8-Stage Production Pipeline

```
Stage 0: Product Understanding → Stage 1: Visual Direction →
Stage 2: Feature Mapping → Stage 3: Storyboarding →
Stage 4: Asset Capture → Stage 5: Shot Implementation →
Stage 6: Sound Design → Stage 7: Acceptance
```

### Stage 0: Product Understanding & Execution Constraints

**Goal**: Fully understand product positioning and constraints before writing code or capturing assets.

### Stage 1: Visual Direction & Styleframe

**Goal**: Lock the color palette, typography, lighting, and motion personality with the cheapest deliverables first.

### Stage 2: Feature-to-Shot Mapping

**Goal**: Select appropriate motion grammar for each feature that must be showcased.

### Stage 3: Storyboarding & Production Gate

**Goal**: Arrange shots into a complete sequence with an energy curve.

### Stage 4: Final Asset Capture

**Goal**: Capture all required real page assets strictly according to the storyboard.

### Stage 5: Shot-by-Shot Implementation

**Goal**: Implement shots frame-by-frame according to the frame-level timeline.

### Stage 6: Sound Design

**Goal**: BGM sets the energy skeleton; SFX pins to motion keyframes beat by beat.

### Stage 7: Acceptance

**Goal**: Formal full-video inspection verifying the output faithfully executes the brief.

## IV. Sound Design Methodology

### 4.1 Core Principle: Picture First

**Sound always comes after picture lock.**

### 4.2 SFX Vocabulary

### 4.3 Anti-Machine-Gun Patterns

1. **Dual-sample alternation**: Rotate two similar samples
2. **Volume step-down**: Linear decay
3. **Curve matching**: Compress intervals
