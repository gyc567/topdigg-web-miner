# ChatGPT Beginner's Guide: Don't Rush to Pay, Boss, Learn the Free Tier First

**The conclusion first:** Today's free ChatGPT tier can already replace half of the "copywriting assistant, translation, data tidying" roles in your company. There is no need to pay for Plus first.

What it can already do for free: **web search** (get real-time information), **upload files and images for analysis**, **generate images**, **use ready-made GPTs**, and **500MB of Library storage**. None of these are "demo versions". They are capabilities that can go straight into your business workflow.

**But the most important part of this article is not "how to sign up". It is three things:**

First, **before signing up you need to know what the official terms really mean for users in mainland China**, so you don't spend money only to find out halfway through registration that it was wasted. Second, **if the free tier is enough, don't rush to buy Plus**. Plus money should be spent on "specific tasks", not on "anxiety". Third, **one ChatGPT Plus account is a company asset, and it should not be used by one person alone**.

This is the current wording from the official help center, not my own guess:

| Capability | Free tier |
|------|-----------|
| Everyday text chat | **Unlimited** (with anti-abuse measures) |

| Web search | ✅ Supported |
| Upload files and images for analysis | ✅ Supported (separate quota) |
| Generate images inside ChatGPT | ✅ Supported (separate quota) |
| Use GPTs other people built | ✅ Supported |

**Three sentences translated into a boss's perspective:**

1. **"Unlimited" means unlimited text chat.** Image generation, data analysis and file analysis have separate quotas, so do not expect to send 200 images a day.
2. **You cannot build your own GPT**, but you can use ones other people built. Enterprise plans can.
3. **Only Plus gets higher quotas, stronger models and priority access.** Official pricing is **20 USD per month** (billed monthly). API fees are billed separately and are not included in Plus.

**This one point must be explained to the boss: ChatGPT Plus and the API are two completely different bills.** Many bosses think that buying Plus gives them API access. It does not. The API is priced separately on the official site.

**Part Two: Three things to settle before registering (they decide whether you waste your time)**

**Item 1: Understand "regional support" first**

OpenAI publishes an official list of "Supported countries and territories", and it states clearly:

> Accessing, or providing access to, the services in countries or regions outside the list **may result in your account being banned or suspended**.

The official team also published a dedicated FAQ explaining the "OpenAI's services are not available in your country" error.

**My judgment, stated plainly:**

Mainland China is not on OpenAI's official support list. Technically you can register and use it normally through an overseas network, but that means **operating at the edge of the official terms**, and the account itself is a fragile asset. **This tutorial gives you step-by-step instructions, but you must be clear about what this is: it is not an officially supported path.**

**What does that mean?** It means the three rules below are not "recommendations", they are "musts":

- One person, one account. **Do not share it with multiple people**;
- Try to stick to one country and one node. **Do not switch IP addresses frequently**;
- **Do not keep important work only inside this account.** Keep a copy of your chat history, files and prompts on your own computer.

**Item 2: Prepare a clean email address**

**Prefer an Outlook / Hotmail address (a Microsoft account).** The reasons are very practical:

- You can later choose "Continue with Microsoft" during registration, one step, with no need to wait for another verification email;
- QQ Mail and 163 Mail occasionally fail to receive verification emails on cross-border mail routes, or the message lands in spam, which wastes time;
- You can register Outlook with a mainland China phone number.

**If you already registered ChatGPT with a phone number, or you use Gmail, you can keep using it. There is no need to start over.**

**Item 3: Decide in advance whether you will use the free tier or a paid tier**

**My suggestion: use the free tier for a full week, then decide whether to pay.**

The reason is that most bosses rush to pay on the day they register, because they are "afraid it is not enough". After one week of real use you will find that **the scenarios that genuinely need Plus are very narrow**: bulk generation, the strongest reasoning, or queuing during peak hours. **The most honest attitude toward paying is: let the actual demand prove it.**

**Part Four: Registering ChatGPT itself (the core steps)**

1. **Confirm the network is connected** (United States / Japan / Singapore node);
2. **Open an incognito window** (Chrome: `Ctrl + Shift + N`; Mac: `Command + Shift + N`). Incognito is used because you may have logged into other accounts in the same browser before, and cookies get mixed up;
3. Go to **`https://chatgpt.com`** in your browser;
4. Click **"Sign up"** in the top right;
5. Choose a signup method. **Recommended order**:
   - **Continue with Microsoft / Continue with Google**, the least effort, done in one step, and you also get a stable account system;
   - or use your Outlook email with a password directly;
6. Go to your email and click the verification link (**remember to check the spam folder**);
7. Fill in your name and date of birth (**use your real age**. OpenAI has age grading, and making up a date can lead to a restricted account. There is no reason to step on this trap);
8. Registration is complete, and you land straight in the chat interface.

**The first thing a new user should do in ChatGPT:**

Click **"New chat"** in the sidebar on the top left, then type in the input box:

```
Hello, please answer in Chinese. I am a small business owner. In three sentences, tell me what you can help me with.
```

**If it replies in Chinese and the answer makes sense, your account is working.**

**Part Five: Quick reference table for common problems (follow it, do not improvise)**

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|------|------|------|
| It says "region not available / not available in your country" | Your current IP falls in an unsupported region, or the browser cached old region data | Switch to a United States / Japan / Singapore node; clear browser cache and cookies; try another browser or an incognito window |
| No verification email arrives | The email went to spam, or cross-border delivery is delayed | Check spam and subscription folders first; retry with Outlook; or use "Continue with Microsoft" to skip email verification |
| It asks for phone verification | Risk control was triggered | Re-register after switching nodes, which usually skips it. **Do not use SMS receiving platforms**, that is where bans are endemic |
| The page spins forever and never loads | Poor node quality or the site is blocked | Switch to another node; turn off "TUN mode" in the system proxy and try again; change browser |
| Registered fine but cannot log in | Cookies or region state are confused | Log in again in an incognito window; confirm you log in with the same method you registered with (Microsoft account vs email) |
| After logging in it says the account is deactivated | You violated the terms of use | Appeal only: log in to chatgpt.com, click Help then Contact Us |

**One last point needs special emphasis: do not buy "ready-made ChatGPT accounts" or "shared accounts".** Those accounts have unknown origins and are shared by multiple people. **A ban is inevitable, and your chat history and uploaded files live in an account someone else can see.** For a company, this is not just wasted money, it is a data leak.

**Part Six: Getting the free tier really working: the 4 scenarios bosses use most**

**Scenario 1: Writing product copy / Moments posts**

```
I am the owner of a small business in [industry]. Please write a Moments post for me that highlights [product selling point]. The tone should be professional yet down to earth, no more than 150 characters.
```

**Scenario 2: Organizing customer questions into standard replies**

```
Below are several questions customers asked me. Please organize them into standard reply scripts and classify them by "price / after-sales / cooperation / other": [paste customer questions]
```

**Scenario 3: Writing weekly and monthly reports**

```
Here is the work I did this week: [list key points]
Please organize it into a concise weekly report with three parts: "completed this week / problems this week / plan for next week".
```

**Scenario 4: Brainstorming a marketing campaign**

```
I want to run a [campaign name] campaign. The budget is [amount], the target audience is [people]. Please give me 5 creative ideas, and for each one spell out: the core mechanic, the resources needed, and the headcount required.
```

**Three more high-frequency uses:**

- **Throw a long document at it and let it read** (upload a PDF), then ask "which clauses of this contract are unfavorable to me";
- **Drop a screenshot in and let it read** (snap a screenshot of your backend data and ask directly "which channel dropped this week");
- **Let web search pull the latest information** (ask "the latest tax policy for small and micro businesses in 2026", which is more accurate than its outdated internal knowledge).

**Three techniques for higher output quality:**

1. **The more specific you are, the more accurate the answer.** "Write some copy" gets you filler, while "write a Moments post about a lipstick shade for 30-year-old women, with one real usage scenario" gets you something usable.
2. **Ask for three versions, not one.** Add a line asking for "3 versions in different styles", then you only have to pick one, which saves time versus editing it yourself.
3. **Know when to start a new chat in a long conversation.** Once the context is full it starts forgetting things, and a "context window full" message does not mean you have to wait for a reset. **Just start a new conversation.**

**Part Seven: Should you buy Plus? Check this table first**

**Official Plus price: 20 USD per month**, billed monthly. Available on the official website and in the official app.

**What Plus adds compared with the free tier:**

| Dimension | Free tier | Plus |
|------|--------|------|
| Quota | Unlimited text, separate limits for tools | Higher quotas, more model and tool choices |
| Peak hours | May have to wait during congestion | Priority access, less interference |
| Reasoning | Basic | Advanced reasoning options, coding, planning, multi-step analysis |
| Response speed | Standard | Faster |
| Voice conversation | Yes | Yes |
| Image generation | Yes | Yes |
| File upload and analysis | Yes | Yes |
| Deep Research | Partially available | Available in more scenarios |
| Build your own GPT | ❌ | ❌ (never allowed on personal accounts) |

**My advice, in this order:**

**Step one: use the free tier for a full week.** Track three things: how many questions you ask per day, when you start finding it slow, and which task types get blocked by quotas. **If you were never blocked in a week, do not pay.**

**Step two: only pay in these two situations.**
- You process large amounts of files, data analysis and image generation every day and are blocked by the separate quotas;
- You are waiting on something that "cannot wait", and peak-hour queuing is hurting delivery.

**Step three: treat Plus as "capacity bought monthly", not as a "membership".** 20 USD is roughly 140 CNY, or under 5 CNY per day. **A boss's calculation should be: if these 5 CNY per day buy back the time of one less assistant, then the money is worth spending.**

**Step four: if you need multiple accounts, do not buy them one by one.** The next section explains a cheaper way.

**Part Eight: Three routes to subscribe from China (ranked by how much hassle they save)**

**Route A: Reseller top-up platforms (least hassle, recommended for absolute beginners)**

Pay directly with Alipay or WeChat, topped up onto your own account, with **no need to provide your password**. Search for "ChatGPT Plus top-up" to find many platforms.

**The criteria for picking a platform (these three filter out most of the traps):**
- The platform has been operating for more than a year and has a checkable shop history;
- It states clearly that it needs "no password, no shared account", and goes through official gift cards or official channels;
- It uses a **US region Apple ID gift card** or **official channel top-ups**, rather than a "shared account pool".

**Risk warning up front: top-up services are in a grey area.** Platforms going out of business, topping up an account that gets flagged, and accounts having their password changed have all genuinely happened. **Top up one month at a time, and do not prepay a year.**

**Route B: Virtual credit card (for people who do not mind the hassle)**

Get a virtual card yourself and bind it as the payment method on the ChatGPT website.

- **Pros**: you are in control, you can cancel anytime, no third-party dependency;
- **Cons**: account opening requires identity verification, and **the ChatGPT subscription itself requires the card's issuing country to match your account region**, which is where people trip up;
- **Good for**: bosses who plan to use it long term and are willing to spend half an hour setting it up.

**Route C: US region Apple ID + gift card (for iPhone users)**

Subscribe through the App Store on iPhone. A **US region Apple ID** can be topped up with gift card balance through official channels.

**This route has the fewest traps but is unfriendly to beginners**: you need to know how to switch Apple ID regions, and the process is tedious. **If you are not a heavy iPhone user, skip this one entirely.**

**Part Nine: What a boss should really do: turn one account into a company asset**

This step is the part of the whole article I most want to tell you about.

**The problem:** in many companies the ChatGPT account is "whoever joins, registers their own", which means nobody knows how much was spent in total, which model is being used, which prompts work well, or who holds the account.

**The tool magpie (I wrote a complete beginner's guide to it in another article) solves this:**

1. You **log in with "Sign in with ChatGPT" in the official ChatGPT app**, and your ChatGPT subscription is recognized as a "model source";
2. Once magpie is installed, your Claude Code, Codex, Cline and OpenClaw tools **can all directly use the models in your ChatGPT subscription**, so one account feeds the whole company;
3. magpie's **Usage page** gives you a billing-grade breakdown: who, which tool, which model, how much was spent;
4. If you want stronger control, you can also enable **LAN sharing**, issue each client an independent key, and set daily, weekly and monthly token and spending limits. **That is a master circuit breaker on the whole company's AI spending.**

**OpenAI explicitly supports this:** Plus users can authorize participating third-party tools to use their quota (Sign in with ChatGPT token sharing), and the official documentation is clear: **using third-party tools consumes quota from your Plus plan and is not charged extra; but this does not allow you to share account credentials or resell access.**

**So the boundary is clear:**
- ✅ You can let tools use your (company's) quota on your own company's computers;
- ❌ You cannot give your account password to others, and you cannot sell the account.

**Operational advice for the boss:**

- Create a **primary company account** (registered with a company email), and pay through the company ledger;
- Connect employee tools through magpie in one place, **instead of having everyone register separately**;
- Review the Usage report once a month and do a cost review.

**This point is the real value of this article: it is not "teaching you to register", it is "turning your AI spending into a manageable ledger".**

**Part Ten: Daily usage discipline (prevents bans, and prevents incidents)**

**At the account level:**

- ✅ One person, one account, never share the password;
- ✅ Fixed node, do not change IP frequently;
- ✅ Keep important work locally (chat history, prompt templates, uploaded files);
- ❌ Do not send sensitive content: customer lists, ID numbers, contract amounts, unpublished financial data;
- ❌ Do not buy ready-made accounts, do not buy shared accounts, do not register through SMS receiving platforms.

**At the data level (this one the boss must take seriously):**

Under ChatGPT's default settings, **your chats may be used for model improvement**. If you handle customer data, contracts or financial reports, **the first thing is not to register an account, it is to classify your company data**: what may be sent to an external model, and what absolutely may not.

**The precondition for cutting costs with AI is knowing what data is allowed to leave the building.** This point matters more than any tutorial, but most tutorials will not tell you.

**Three policy suggestions at the company level:**

1. **Do not let employees run company business on personal accounts.** This is where every AI incident starts.
2. **Prompt templates should accumulate into assets.** Write effective prompts into files stored in the company knowledge base, instead of relying on each person's memory.
3. **Do an account inventory every quarter.** Who is using it, which one, how much was spent, and is it still valid.

**Part Eleven: A one-week rollout plan (do not do it all at once)**

**Day 1:** Register the account and get 3 real tasks done on the free tier (write copy, read a document, look up one piece of latest information). **The goal is not to master it, it is to know whether it actually works for you.**

**Day 2:** Write the 3 most repetitive tasks in your company into prompt templates and save them as files.

**Day 3:** Decide whether to buy Plus. **Follow the three steps in Part Seven. Do not act on impulse.**

**Week 2:** Connect the ChatGPT account into magpie, unify the model source for Claude Code / Codex / Cline, turn on the Usage report, and see the full picture of your company's AI spending for the first time.

**Week 3:** Give each team member an independent key and a monthly limit. **From that day on, AI spending is no longer a fuzzy account.**

**Final summary**

The ChatGPT story is half technology and half management. The technology half takes one afternoon to learn. The management half decides whether, a year from now, you "saved money with AI" or "AI cost a pile of money and nobody can say where it went".

**Three sentences for you:**

**First, the free tier is already enough.** Web search, file analysis, image generation, GPTs, use it for a full week before thinking about paying.

**Second, keep the discipline at signup.** One account per person, one node from start to finish, never buy shared accounts. This is not superstition, it is whether your account survives three months.

**Third, turn the account into a company asset, not a personal toy.** One primary company account + one unified gateway + one usage report is the correct way for both bosses and employees to use AI.

**The first two sentences decide whether you get banned. The third decides whether your AI spending is a manageable cost or a mystery.**
