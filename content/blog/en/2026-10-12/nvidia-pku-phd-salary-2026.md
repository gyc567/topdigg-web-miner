A screenshot has been making rounds in tech circles: **Nvidia offered a Peking University fresh PhD graduate an annual package of approximately 2.9 million RMB in the first year**.

Is this for real? Let's dig in.

## 1. The 2.9M Offer: Real or Fake?

First, the honest answer: **we can't 100% verify this screenshot**. It's from a social media submission, the original post is no longer traceable, and neither Nvidia nor the individual confirmed it.

But that doesn't stop us from analyzing the broader phenomenon.

The 2.9M figure, while eye-catching, isn't entirely unreasonable. Here's what we know from verifiable sources:

- **Nvidia 2026 Campus Recruitment**: Undergraduate ~738K RMB total package, Master's 800K+, PhD well over 1M, core positions with Special Offers reaching several million
- **Tencent's "Qingyun Plan"**: Circulated salary standard of 1M to 3M+ RMB, uncapped
- **ByteDance's "Top Seed"**: 2026 top AI talent program, recruiting ~30 PhDs focused on LLMs and ML systems

Top AI PhDs from elite Tsinghua and Peking labs entering Big Tech's model R&D projects are already earning "several million RMB is not uncommon" in their first year — this is from First Financial, citing sources familiar with Peking University PhD employment.

So **2.9M isn't the norm, but it's not fantasy either**. It's more like an extreme data point — the intersection of a top candidate, a top company, and special negotiation circumstances.

## 2. The Talent War: How Valuable Are These PhDs Really?

A recruiter friend put it bluntly: "Recently been busy headhunting Tsinghua-Peking Class of 2027 PhDs."

Behind this is a brutal supply-demand reality:

**Supply**: Top AI PhDs from Tsinghua and Peking's elite labs — Computer Science, AI, Interdisciplinary Studies, Mathematics — are extremely limited in number. A lab might produce just a dozen PhDs per cohort, with perhaps three to five truly elite.

**Demand**: Global tech giants, AI startups, and top research institutions are all competing. Nvidia, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta, Anthropic, Alibaba, ByteDance, Tencent — whoever signs these people first gains a decisive edge in the next phase of AI competition.

One AI startup founder, Wang Hao (pseudonym), told First Financial something cutting:

> "Large models are now the most valuable track, and they are the most valuable people of this era."

He gave an example: Alibaba's Qwen model holds hundreds of thousands of compute cards, representing nearly $10 billion in investment. If even one PhD graduate can help optimize chip utilization by just 1%, their multi-million salary is practically loose change by comparison.

## 3. How Intense Are Big Tech's Recruitment Programs?

This talent war has evolved far beyond simply outbidding each other on salary.

**Tencent "Qingyun Plan"**: Targets global top technical talent, offering customized training, core business roles, and "highly competitive compensation." Circulated figures: 1M–3M+ RMB, uncapped.

**ByteDance "Top Seed"**: 2026 LLM top talent campus program, planning to recruit ~30 outstanding PhDs. Their pitch: "Find the top 5% in the LLM field, do what the other 95% cannot."

**Nvidia**: High base salary plus RSU (4-year vesting) and signing bonus. Core positions can reach several million with Special Offers.

Even Master's graduates aren't far behind. Peking University Master's graduates are reportedly earning **several thousand RMB per day** — at 22 working days per month, that's easily 60-70K+ monthly.

## 4. The Hidden Worry: Will Talent Supply Overtake Demand in 5 Years?

Yang Ling, Assistant Professor at Peking University's International Center for Machine Learning, raised a thought-provoking concern:

> "Many Tsinghua-Peking PhDs go directly to tech giants for core tasks, directly replacing the previous generation of tech talent. This is quite dangerous."

His judgment: **In 5 years, AI talent won't be as scarce as today**.

Reasons:
1. University talent supply will steadily increase
2. Model companies will develop their own talent pipelines
3. Industry growth may hit a ceiling, dampening demand

This doesn't mean he's definitely right — but it reminds us: today's astronomical salaries are a product of a specific historical moment, not eternal pricing.

## 5. Is There Still a Chance for Ordinary People?

Every time news like this surfaces, the most common reaction among regular people is: **anxiety**.

Top talent earning tens of millions while you can't even find the door. This gap may only be widening.

But flip the perspective: AI is reshaping every industry, and that reshaping has barely begun. The PhDs at the top today are the people who chose computer science 20 years ago. Those entering AI today might become the backbone of 5-10 years from now.

One line that's both painful and real:

> You might not care about AI, but AI will definitely care about your job.

The difference is whether you're the one being cared about — or the one being cared about by the one being cared about.
