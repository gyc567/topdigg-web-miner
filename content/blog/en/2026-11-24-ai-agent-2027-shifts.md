# Stop Using AI to Do Stupid Things Faster: An AI Company CEO Breaks Down the 5 Shifts for 2027

**Bottom line:** The most valuable thing in this 9-minute video is not the list of tools, it is five shifts in thinking. They turn the fake question of "should we use AI" into the real question of "which bottleneck do we solve first."

The transcript comes from Dan Shipper, the CEO of an overseas AI company. What he says can be put in a single line: **most people are using AI, just to do stupid things faster.** Doing the wrong thing less quickly does not depend on a stronger model, it depends on five judgments you can change today.

First, here is what those five shifts are, then how a boss should use them, and finally the three places that are most easily misread.

---

**1. Shift 1: Don't use AI to do stupid things faster, find the constraint first**

His first core point is uncomfortable to hear: a lot of people out there are using AI, but using it the wrong way, so they do not grow and they do not make money. **Most people are using AI, just to do stupid things faster.**

That is not a way to win. They will not do the wrong thing faster, they will do the right thing.

How do you do it right? Find your **primary constraint** first. His line is blunt: any business, at any moment, has only one primary constraint.

Note that: **one**. Not five.

Find it, then use AI to solve it, and only then does the whole team share one direction.

**What this means for a boss:** Is the most valuable time in your company going into solving the real bottleneck, or into making something that should not exist at all run faster?

If the processes your team runs every day only make some already redundant step run more smoothly, then AI is just helping you move faster toward the wrong destination.

**Actionable step:** Take a sheet of paper and write down the one step your company is most stuck on (not five). Write only one. Then let AI solve only that one.

---

**2. Shift 2: You plus AI beats your team plus AI**

This one is the hardest to swallow and the easiest to misread, so let's look at the original words first.

He says he has coached many excellent business owners, with revenue in the range of 10 million, 25 million and 50 million. He taught them to use AI to capture large volumes of demand, automate sales, process and delivery, and thereby create profit. And yet these people still think first: "then I should equip my team as well."

His answer is: **your team is moving at your pace. The bottleneck is you, not your team.**

So he encourages bosses to carve out dedicated time in their calendar, for learning and for growing, to become the person the team needs.

**What this means for a boss:** This is not telling you to blame your team, and it is not telling you to stop using your team. It means **your rate of adoption is the ceiling on your team's rate of adoption.** If you do not move, the team cannot get moving.

One concrete question to ask yourself: **Is your team moving at your pace, or have you already fallen behind it?**

**Actionable step:** Track how much time you spent on AI this week, and how much time you spent on "researching which part of my team AI could take over," separately. If the second one is zero, your team probably is too.

---

**3. Shift 3: Role design is worth an order of magnitude more than prompt engineering**

This is the most technical part of what he says, and also the most useful one for a boss.

He says the whole AI world has changed: now that agents are hosted by some of the large models (he mentioned a few new hosted agent formats, both domestic and international), most people still cannot easily put them to work. They see the same thing with products like Muse and Instinct.

Someone asked him: "How do I make my AI do smarter things?"

His answer is: **Going forward, who would you hire to win back the most energy and time for your business?**

A role. And then he told the person to go define what that person does.

Once the person finished describing the role, he took out his phone, talked to his own agent, and within a few minutes it was clear: **everything he had just done was defining a role, not writing a prompt.**

**What this means for a boss:** Prompt engineering gets AI to do one thing. Role design is **defining a position**. The first is tool usage, the second is organizational design.

And here is a cause and effect most bosses have not noticed: **the reason your company cannot grow is often not a shortage of people, it is that you have not worked out which roles you actually need.** If you cannot say which roles you need, you cannot say which parts can be handed off, and you cannot say which parts must stay with people.

**Actionable step:** Take a sheet of paper and write down the most expensive step in your business (the one that burns the most labor), then write a single sentence: what result should this role produce each day? Do not write what actions to take, write what result to produce.

---

**4. Shift 4: Data equity, the moat is not the model, it is data quality**

This is the one with the highest commercial value in the whole segment.

In AI, equity ultimately comes down to data quality. **Having the same models and tools as everyone else does not put you in a position of advantage.**

So where is the opportunity? In cleaning and verifying data, so that it can be fed into a model, so that an idea can be carried all the way through to completion with no human involvement at any step.

He gave an example with real detail: a friend of his manages the entire sales process. Before a call even starts, there is already a form filled out, then the sales conversation, the signing, the onboarding, the entry into the system.

He asked: do you have examples of these steps? The answer was: no.

His judgment is: **if you have not defined your data, cleaned your data and prepared the examples, then you have no value, and no equity value to speak of.**

Every company should ask itself: how do I build data asset value inside my own company?

**What this means for a boss:** This one directly answers the question of what is most valuable in the age of AI. It is not the model, everyone has models. **It is your data being cleaner than your peers'.**

And the bar to entry is low enough to be almost funny: it needs no compute and no algorithms, it just means turning the scattered, dirty, duplicated data you already hold into samples a machine can execute.

But almost no company has done it, so right now it is a windfall.

**Actionable step:** Pick the one step in your business that depends most on "experience," record the process a veteran uses to handle it, and turn it into 10 examples with inputs and outputs. This is the first step of building a data asset.

---

**5. Shift 5: The learning loop, without a feedback loop it is not an agent**

The last shift is the key that ties the previous four together.

People kept telling him: "Dan, I have a bot. I have a bot."

He said: no, you do not. What you have is automation.

**The difference between automation and an agent comes down to whether there is a learning loop.**

A learning loop works like this: define a goal, generate a result, receive feedback at some step, send the feedback back into the original prompt, run the steps with the new knowledge, and so each round is better than the last.

He gave a very concrete example: have your agent run some analysis, send the analysis to Slack, let the team give feedback on that analysis, then have the agent monitor the post it published itself, collect the feedback, treat it as a chance to upgrade itself, and upgrade its rules, steps, process and quality.

**If you do this once a week, that is 52 times a year, and it keeps getting better.**

**What this means for a boss:** This is the easiest one to understand and the easiest one to skip. Most companies adopt an AI tool and then nothing happens after that, because **nobody defined what counts as winning.**

Feedback is not likes and reposts, it is "this analysis helped me make a decision" versus "I did not use this analysis at all." If you cannot say what counts as winning, you cannot feed the feedback back, and the agent stays where it is.

**Actionable step:** Set one criterion for your AI process: was this output actually used to make a decision? Count once a week, and put the outputs that missed the bar next to their prompts. This is the cheapest single improvement in your whole system.

---

**6. The three places that are easiest to read backwards**

The first five are what he said. The next three are my own judgment, and you are free to disagree.

**First, "you plus AI beats your team plus AI" does not mean layoffs.** This is the reading most likely to cause damage. His actual point is that you should first become the person your team needs. That is a demand that you upgrade yourself first, not an instruction to cut people first. Get the order wrong, cut headcount first and then stop using AI yourself, and the business collapses faster.

**Second, the barrier to "role engineering" is not AI, it is management.** Plenty of people hear about role design and immediately want to go buy a tool. The reality is: if your company cannot say what a given role should produce each day, then every agent you buy is just automating the chaos.

**Third, "data equity" sounds like strategy but is manual work in practice.** Everybody wants a moat, but a moat is not thought up, it is organized. It takes someone sitting down and turning three years of customer records, quotes and delivery logs from "a pile of files" into "samples a machine can read." There is no technical difficulty in it, but almost nobody does it, which is exactly why it is valuable.

---

**7. His final line may be the most valuable part of the whole segment**

After going through the five shifts, he made a bigger call: every company is going to become an AI software company.

But he was careful to clarify: this is not SaaS, not the concept of "software as a service." **It is turning the service you provide into software.**

The concrete path is: get good enough at solving problems for the market, then deliver on the backend with AI at greater speed and higher quality, which lifts your gross margin, because you do not have to hire a large number of people.

**Let your people keep doing the work that needs people, and let AI handle the work that happens on a computer.**

The value of this line for a boss is that it completes the missing bridge between "using AI" and "making money." The first five shifts are about direction, this one is about the **financial result**: gross margin.

If you want one metric to judge whether your company is using AI correctly, I would make it gross margin. If gross margin did not move after you started using AI, then no matter how much automation you run, you are just working overtime for yourself.

---

**8. A summary, and three things you can do tomorrow**

The core of these 9 minutes is really a **sorting method**: use AI to find your primary constraint, define clearly which roles you need, clean up your data, and finally give it a feedback loop.

The most counterintuitive and also most important point is that it does not treat the model as the key. Everyone assumes the key is the tools or the models, but it is really how you read that information and how you set priorities. That is what decides whether any of this is useful to you at all.

His call for 2027 is that this year will become a dividing line, separating the companies that decide to go professional from the amateur players who end up going under.

I think that call holds, but for a plainer reason than the one he gave: **the difference between professional and amateur is not which tools you use, it is whether you put your effort into that single bottleneck.**

Three things you can do tomorrow:

**1. Find your single bottleneck, write down only one.** Then let AI solve only that one. Do not make a list.

**2. Write your most expensive step as a role spec.** State only what result it produces, not the actions.

**3. Set one "what counts as winning" line for your AI process.** Without it, your agent is just automation.

Those three things together take less than 2 hours and cost nothing. And once they are done, when you look at the new agent products, your standard for judging them is completely different: **you no longer ask "can it do this," you ask "can it solve my bottleneck."**

That difference is the difference between amateur and professional.
